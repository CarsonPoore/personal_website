# Research: icp-founders

## Summary
Early-stage founders (pre-seed to seed, plus bootstrapped SaaS/service founders) almost never buy "a marketing agency" — they buy a strategic operator who thinks like an owner: positioning/messaging sprints, GTM plans, a first repeatable channel, and fractional CMO advisory. Their defining pain is distribution ("I built it and nobody came"), founder-led sales hitting its ceiling around $1-2M ARR, and prior agency burns that produced activity instead of revenue. They evaluate consultants almost entirely through founder-peer referrals, specific stage-matched proof, and speed to first visible result. Indianapolis has a real, verifiable ecosystem to anchor local credibility — High Alpha (B2B SaaS venture studio), gener8tor's gBETA Indiana (free 7-week accelerator), Elevate Ventures, and TechPoint (under CICP; new CEO Eric Christopher as of April 2026) — with Indy startups raising roughly $892M across ~147 deals in 2025. For this ICP, lead with Strategy (the $3,000 engagement as the natural entry) backed by Coaching & Growth (fractional CMO retainer) as the continuation, with Technical (AI automation, analytics) as the differentiator agencies can't match.

## Key findings
- Fractional executive demand is real and growing: the fractional model is driven by full-time CMO cost (fractional CMOs typically cost 40-50% less than a full-time hire) and short average CMO tenure (~4.1 years), per 2026 market coverage (data-mania.com, marketful.com). One source cites a ~$5.7B fractional executive market in 2026 — treat that single-source figure as directional, not citable.
- The core fractional-CMO-vs-agency distinction founders repeat: 'a fractional CMO thinks like an owner and owns outcomes (revenue, GTM plan); an agency owns deliverables and channel metrics' (data-mania.com GTM decision framework). This is the exact wedge CPC's Coaching & Growth practice occupies.
- Typical fractional CMO hiring trigger: $500K-$2M ARR when founders need strategy beyond tactical agency work; sweet spot $2M-$10M post-PMF (upgrowth.in, dev.to 2026 explainer). CPC's $1,200-$4,800 retainers undercut typical fractional CMO rates ($5K-$15K/mo commonly cited), which is a genuine pricing advantage worth stating plainly.
- Founder-led sales breaks around $1-2M ARR, or more precisely 'when you personally close 70-80% of new business and burn 15+ hours a week doing it' (SaaStr). The #1 failure mode of the first marketing/sales hire: the founder never documented a repeatable process before handing it off — a consultant who documents the playbook is the bridge.
- Agency skepticism is structural, not anecdotal: SaaStr's canonical line is 'one thing that never works: hiring a bunch of agencies instead of a VP of marketing.' Cervin Ventures: the agency-client relationship for early-stage startups is 'structurally set up to produce the wrong outcomes' — agencies deliver what's easy to measure, not what moves the business.
- Specific agency complaints founders voice: rigid contracts with committed spend levels before the startup knows which channel works; attention that dips with price; reports full of activity but no baseline or target number; and the most expensive mistake — hiring a performance agency before product-market fit (Entrepreneur, techuk, growthmk).
- What early-stage founders actually buy: positioning and messaging sprints, ICP/persona definition, GTM plans, and a first repeatable channel — typically 2-week sprints priced $1,500-$3,000+ (Contra, Maven, columncontent.com). CPC's $3,000 strategy engagement sits exactly at the verified market price point for this deliverable.
- Diagnosis that recurs across sources: 'most startups don't stall because the product doesn't work; they stall because the messaging is unclear, sales is guessing, and nobody's aligned on who the product is for' — positioning problems masquerade as growth problems.
- Distribution is the verified #1 bootstrapped-founder pain: multiple Indie Hackers threads titled variations of 'I built a SaaS that got 0 paying customers at launch — distribution was the real problem all along' and 'why is getting the first 100 users so much harder than building the app.' One founder's fix: flipping to 90% distribution / 10% building.
- How founders evaluate consultants: (1) founder-peer referrals first — 'which firm would you hire again, and at what stage'; (2) specific stage-matched proof — named companies, actual outcomes; strong consultants offer references before being asked; (3) they distinguish 'advice-givers' from 'growth-builders' and want someone matched to their stage, not a Series C resume (recruitingfromscratch.com, a16z, NCFP).
- AI automation services startups verifiably pay for: lead identification/qualification and personalized outreach automation, 24/7 AI chat support, customer onboarding automation, invoice/AP processing, document routing, and data entry/validation — plus 'AI strategy' engagements to pick high-value use cases (designveloper, codewave, biz4group, datumquest). Workflow automation market ~$26B in 2026 (single source; directional).
- Indianapolis ecosystem verified as of late 2025/2026: High Alpha — venture studio + VC focused on B2B SaaS (name current); gener8tor runs gBETA Indiana, a free 7-week accelerator with public pitch night (name current); Elevate Ventures — active early-stage investor (name current); TechPoint — operates under CICP (Central Indiana Corporate Partnership), new president/CEO Eric Christopher effective April 7, 2026, succeeding Ting Gootee; runs the Mira Awards and annual Indiana Venture Report.
- Indianapolis startups raised approximately $892M across 147 deals in 2025, concentrated in SaaS, healthcare tech, and logistics (superscout.co); the $250M Next Level Indiana Fund and repeat investors (High Alpha, Elevate) now lead later rounds, keeping growth-stage firms in-state. TechPoint's 2025 Venture Report described the year as 'recalibration and renewed focus on long-term value creation' — i.e., capital efficiency is the mood; founders are spending carefully.
- Timing/fit nuance for CPC: pre-PMF founders should NOT buy paid-media retainers (verified consensus: 'ads are for scale, not for starting'), which means the honest move — telling a too-early founder what not to buy — is itself the trust-builder this audience responds to, and matches CPC's no-pushy-urgency voice.
- 'Random acts of marketing' is an established founder-facing term of art (Authentic Brand was founded specifically to fix it; Social Media Today series) and 'leaky bucket' is standard startup vocabulary for churn before PMF — both safe to use on the pathway page as recognized language.

## Actionable
- Pathway page angle: lead with the distribution/founder-led-sales pain, not services. Working headline territory: 'You built the thing. Now let's get it to people.' Frame CPC as the anti-agency: one strategist who thinks like an owner, owns outcomes not deliverables, and builds a repeatable playbook the founder can eventually hand to a hire. Name the stage explicitly (pre-seed to seed, bootstrapped, roughly first dollar to ~$2M ARR) because founders filter consultants by stage-match first.
- Lead with practice #3 (Strategy) as the front door for this ICP — the $3,000 strategy engagement maps exactly to what this market verifiably buys (positioning/ICP/messaging/GTM sprint, market-priced $1,500-$3,000+). Position practice #4 (Coaching & Growth / fractional CMO retainer) as the natural continuation, and note plainly that $1,200-$4,800/mo is well under typical fractional CMO rates. Technical (AI agents, analytics, CRM) is the differentiator to weave in — 'your strategist can also build the automation' — not the lead.
- Top objections to answer on-page, in order: (1) 'We got burned by an agency' — answer with the outcomes-vs-deliverables distinction and the 8-client cap as structural proof of attention; (2) 'Are we too early for this?' — answer honestly: if you're pre-PMF, don't buy ads or a retainer; the strategy engagement tells you what to do yourself first (this candor is the conversion mechanism); (3) 'Why not just hire someone?' — a full-time marketer at this stage costs more and still needs direction; buy the playbook first, hire against it later; (4) 'Have you worked with founders at my stage?' — show stage-specific proof, named where possible, offered before being asked.
- Proof requirements this ICP demands: specific stage-matched outcomes (named clients, actual numbers), peer-referral energy ('would hire again'), and speed — show what happens in the first 2 weeks. Fabricated or vague 'results' will read instantly as agency-speak; if real founder case studies don't exist yet, use the nonprofit/local results with an honest bridge line rather than inventing startup proof.
- Anchor local credibility with verified ecosystem names, current as of Oct 2026: High Alpha (B2B SaaS venture studio), gBETA Indiana by gener8tor (free 7-week accelerator), Elevate Ventures, TechPoint (under CICP; CEO Eric Christopher since April 2026; Mira Awards; annual Indiana Venture Report). Safe local stat: Indy startups raised ~$892M across ~147 deals in 2025 (superscout.co — cite if used). Do NOT claim affiliation with any of these orgs; reference them as the ecosystem CPC founders live in ('If you've pitched at a gBETA night or sat in a High Alpha office...').
- For the AI/automation section of this pathway, list only verifiably-bought services: lead qualification and outreach automation, AI chat support, onboarding automation, invoice/document processing, and analytics/GA4 instrumentation. Frame as 'the ops work that stops you being the bottleneck' — founders buy time back, not 'AI transformation.'
- Avoid on this page: 'funnel', 'leverage', 'ROI-driven', 'growth hacking', 'skyrocket', any urgency mechanics, and any unverified market-size statistics. The two single-source figures ($5.7B fractional market, $26B workflow automation market) should not appear on the site.
- Keep the five-step method mapped to founder language: Conviction → 'why this product, said clearly'; Your people → 'who it's actually for (ICP)'; A sharp offer → 'positioning and the first repeatable channel'; The experience → 'onboarding and the leaky bucket'; Word of mouth → 'founder-peer referrals, which is also how you found me.'

- I built it and nobody came. / Launched to zero customers.
- Distribution was the real problem all along.
- Getting the first 100 users is so much harder than building the app.
- Founder-led sales is breaking — the company only grows when I personally get on the call.
- I'm closing 80% of deals myself and burning 15 hours a week doing it.
- We tried an agency and got reports, not revenue.
- The reports look busy but I can't point to a single customer they brought us.
- We're doing random acts of marketing.
- Nobody's aligned on who this is actually for.
- Our messaging is unclear and sales is guessing.
- We have a leaky bucket — people sign up and disappear.
- We tried ads and nothing worked. / We burned $10K on ads before we had product-market fit.
- I know we need marketing but I can't afford a real CMO.
- I don't have time to manage freelancers and agencies on top of everything else.
- I need someone who's done this at my stage, not a Fortune 500 resume.
- Who would you hire again? (how founders actually pick consultants — peer referral)
- I need a repeatable playbook I can eventually hand to a hire.
- Everything in our ops is held together with duct tape and spreadsheets.
- I'm the bottleneck for everything.
