# Research: icp-nonprofits

## Summary
Central Indiana nonprofits ($100K–$5M budgets) are squeezed between rising demand and a shrinking donor base: giving dollars rose ~5% in 2025 while donor counts fell ~3.6%, and the smallest donors ($1–$100) dropped ~11% YoY — small orgs feel this hardest because growth is concentrating in large organizations. The typical buyer is an executive director or development director wearing marketing, fundraising, and comms hats simultaneously, with a board that scrutinizes any spend that looks like 'overhead.' The Google Ad Grant ($10K/month in free search ads, verified active in 2026) is the single best service hook: the average self-managed account uses only ~$300–$800 of the $10,000 and accounts routinely get suspended over the 5% CTR rule, while professionally managed accounts average ~$8,650/month — so Ad Grants management, email/CRM cleanup, and storytelling are the services that map directly to their pains. Indiana is an unusually fundable state (Lilly Endowment's GIFT IX put up to $271.5M into community foundations; United Way of Central Indiana runs capacity-building grants up to $50K that can pay for exactly this kind of help), which gives the pathway page a credible 'the money to fix this may already exist' angle.

## Key findings
- Donor base is shrinking while dollars grow: 2025 Fundraising Effectiveness Project data shows total giving up ~5% YoY but donor count down 3.6%, continuing a decline every year since 2019 (Nonprofit Times / NonprofitPRO / FEP Q1 2025).
- Smallest donors are vanishing fastest: the $1–$100 donor segment (57% of all donors in Q1 2025) shrank 11.1% YoY — exactly the segment community-sized nonprofits depend on (FEP Q1 2025 via pr.com/NonprofitPRO).
- Growth is concentrating in midsize/large orgs; small nonprofits face disproportionate pressure from limited capacity, acquisition struggles, and inconsistent planning — the $100K–$5M band is the stressed band (NonprofitPRO 2025).
- 81% of nonprofits surveyed in 2025 struggled to raise enough to cover costs; 36% ended their fiscal year with an operating deficit; 33% reported government funding disruptions in early 2025 (Independent Sector).
- Google Ad Grants is confirmed active in 2026: up to $10,000/month in free Google Search text ads for verified 501(c)(3)s via Google for Nonprofits, no cap on number of participating orgs (Elevation, Donorbox, Getting Attention 2026 guides).
- Ad Grants compliance is the trap: 5% minimum CTR, monthly login, conversion tracking required, no single-word keywords, search-only text ads — accounts get suspended constantly for CTR and inactivity (Elevation 2026 guide, Levitate 2026 guide).
- Ad Grants underutilization is the sales wedge: average self-managed account uses only ~$300 of the $10,000/month (~3%); unmanaged accounts average under $800/month, while professionally managed accounts averaged ~$8,650/month with 68% exceeding $9,000 (Getting Attention; Redefine/Higgens Jan 2026 management data — cite as industry-reported figures).
- Nonprofit email still works: ~28.6% average open rate, 3.29% click rate, ~$2.40 raised per subscriber per year (up from $1.87), email = ~11% of online revenue (M+R Benchmarks 2025 / NPTechForGood 2025).
- Tech stack pain is fragmentation, not absence: donor data sits in 'five systems that don't talk' — spreadsheets, email tool, payment processor, event platform; orgs lack a trusted 360° view of constituents, and the CRM adoption gap between large and small nonprofits is widening (Centric Consulting, Bitcot 2025, AMT Lab).
- 58% of nonprofits were implementing AI in 2025 (up from 44% in 2024), mostly for donor management and admin automation — but small orgs can't implement at the same pace, creating a digital divide Carson's Technical practice can bridge (Bitcot 2025 nonprofit tech trends).
- Volunteering is in long-term decline (~30% of Americans pre-COVID to ~28% in 2023; BLS shows decade-long slide), and 75% of nonprofits say volunteers are essential to operations — orgs specifically struggle to find skilled, long-term, weekday-available volunteers (Census/AmeriCorps via ActivateGood, Bloomerang 2025).
- The buyer is a hat-stacker: in small nonprofits one person is often ED + development director + marketer + volunteer manager at once; boards frequently push EDs to prioritize fundraising over marketing, and some board members are openly reluctant to 'mix marketing with mission' (management.org, SSIR 'No Market for Marketing', BoardEffect).
- How they buy: formal RFPs exist but are widely criticized within the sector; for smaller engagements nonprofits prefer interviewing 2–3 pre-vetted/referred consultants, want a written scope with goals and timetable, and choose on trust and sector fluency more than on price (Nonprofit Times '6 Steps to Picking a Consultant', ASU Lodestar, NonprofitPRO).
- Indiana is a uniquely funder-rich environment: ~44,000+ registered Indiana nonprofits; Lilly Endowment dominates the state's philanthropy (~$11B granted 2018–2025) and announced GIFT IX with up to $271.5M for Indiana community foundations; a community foundation serves every one of Indiana's 92 counties with ~$5.4B combined assets (Lilly Endowment Oct 2025, nonprofit.indiana.edu, opengrants).
- Central Indiana's sector contributes ~$43B annually and employs ~1 in 10 private-sector workers; CICF has granted ~$204M (2019–2024); United Way of Central Indiana awards capacity-building grants up to $50K and gave $130K in 2025 capacity grants — i.e., third-party money exists that can pay for marketing/tech capacity help (CICF, UWCI Capacity Fund pages).
- Indianapolis is home to the IU Lilly Family School of Philanthropy and the Indiana Nonprofits Project — the national center of nonprofit-sector research sits in Carson's own market, which the pathway page can nod to as local sector literacy.

## Actionable
- Pathway page angle: lead with 'You're doing mission-critical work on a shoestring, and marketing always falls to whoever has ten spare minutes.' Name the hat-stacking ED/development director directly. Frame marketing spend as mission delivery, not overhead: more donors found, more volunteers recruited, more people served — pre-empt board skepticism with a line they can repeat to their board.
- Make the Google Ad Grant the hero offer and the foot in the door: '$10,000 a month in free Google ads exists for your organization — most nonprofits that have it use about $300 of it.' Offer a free Ad Grant checkup (eligible? active? suspended? how much of the $10K used last month?) as the low-risk entry point. Be concrete about compliance pains in plain words: the 5% click rule, the monthly login rule, why accounts get shut off.
- Service priority for this pathway, in order: (1) Google Ad Grants setup + management, (2) email + donor CRM cleanup ('one list everyone trusts' — fix the three-spreadsheets problem, map to GoHighLevel/data-management capability), (3) storytelling and brand (impact stories, the case for support, website copy that explains what you actually do), (4) GA4/analytics and simple dashboards so the ED can show the board real numbers. AI/automation is sold as 'a small team that runs like a bigger one,' never as replacing the human relationships that drive giving.
- Present the reduced rate as respect, not charity: name it something like 'nonprofit rate' or 'mission rate' with one plain sentence — 'Nonprofits get a reduced rate. Not because the work is smaller — because the budgets are, and the work matters.' Same scope, same cap, same 3-month minimum as every client; never imply a lesser tier. Avoid 'discount' and 'giving back' framing; research shows nonprofits respond to partnership language and resent charity framing.
- Add a 'this may already be fundable' block: point to United Way of Central Indiana's Capacity Fund (up to $50K capacity-building grants), community foundation capacity grants (one serves every Indiana county), and offer to help write the capacity-grant case for hiring this engagement — ties the $3,000 strategy engagement to money they can actually get.
- Use verified numbers sparingly on-page, in plain voice: donors down 3.6% while dollars rose (FEP 2025), average Ad Grant account uses ~$300 of $10,000, ~28.6% nonprofit email open rate, email ≈ 11% of online revenue (M+R 2025). Attribute them ('industry data from M+R / the Fundraising Effectiveness Project'). Do not invent local stats.
- Match how they buy: they pick consultants by referral and trust, not RFP. Pathway CTA should be a conversation, not a proposal — 'Talk it through with Carson' — and the page should prove sector fluency (restricted funds, stewardship, lapsed donors, board dynamics) in its own copy so sector literacy is shown, not claimed.
- Local SEO targets for this pathway: 'Google Ad Grant management Indianapolis', 'nonprofit marketing consultant Indianapolis', 'nonprofit website help Indiana'. Mention central Indiana anchors naturally (Lilly Endowment's statewide ecosystem, United Way of Central Indiana, community foundations in all 92 counties) to signal rootedness without name-dropping as fake affiliation.
- Map the five-step method to nonprofit language on this page: Conviction = your mission and why it matters now; Your people = donors, volunteers, and the people you serve are three different audiences; A sharp offer = a clear case for support and a clear volunteer ask; The experience = what happens after someone gives once (stewardship, thank-yous, email); Word of mouth = your board, volunteers, and donors telling the story for you.
- Respect the volunteer angle as a co-equal pain with donations: a volunteer-recruitment section (declining volunteerism, 75% of orgs depend on volunteers) differentiates the page from every agency that only talks about donors — and Ad Grants + email serve volunteer recruitment too.
- Sensitive-segment note for churches-adjacent orgs: Google for Nonprofits has its own verification path and policy nuances for faith-based organizations — say 'faith-based and community organizations welcome' and verify individual eligibility during the audit rather than promising it on the page.

## Language bank
- We're wearing too many hats — there's no one here whose actual job is marketing.
- Our donors are aging out and we don't know how to reach the next generation.
- The board wants to see every dollar go to programs, not overhead.
- We can't afford to look like we're spending money on ourselves.
- We have the Google Grant but honestly nobody's touched it in months.
- Our donor data is in three different spreadsheets and nobody trusts any of them.
- We're drowning in the day-to-day — the newsletter goes out when someone has time.
- Donor fatigue is real — our year-end appeal did less than last year.
- We keep losing lapsed donors and we have no system to win them back.
- We need help telling our story — people don't understand what we actually do.
- We can't compete with the big organizations for attention or for staff.
- Everything is restricted funds — there's no money for capacity.
- We need board buy-in before we can spend anything on this.
- Our development director just left and everything lived in her head.
- We rely on volunteers, but recruiting them is harder every year.
- GivingTuesday and year-end are our whole fundraising calendar.
- Grant reporting eats up the time we'd spend on outreach.
- We know we should be doing email better, we just don't know where to start.
- Is this something a capacity-building grant could cover?
- We don't want a vendor, we want someone who gets the nonprofit world.
