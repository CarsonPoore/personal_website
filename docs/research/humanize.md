# Research: humanize

## Summary
Research across Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" field guide (WikiProject AI Cleanup), 2025-2026 copywriter commentary, and LLM-detection literature converges on a consistent set of AI tells: formulaic contrast constructions ("It's not just X—it's Y"), a stable high-density vocabulary (delve, landscape, robust, seamless, elevate, testament, tapestry), significance inflation, rule-of-three stacking, summary endings, vague attribution, hedged non-claims, and uniform sentence rhythm. Human-reading copy does the opposite: real numbers and place names, first-person conviction, concrete verbs, varied sentence length, and occasional rule-breaking. One caution from detection research: statistical "burstiness" is no longer a reliable machine detector against 2026 models (near coin-flip AUROC in one 2026 analysis), but uniform rhythm remains a strong perceptual tell for human readers — which is what matters for carsonpoore.com. The checklist below is ordered by how strongly each pattern signals AI and is written so a copy-QA agent can apply it mechanically with grep-able cues.

## Key findings
- Wikipedia's WikiProject AI Cleanup maintains the most comprehensive field guide of AI tells ('Signs of AI writing'); tech press in late 2025 called it the best available guide. Its top content-level signals: undue significance inflation ('stands as a testament', 'plays a vital/pivotal role', 'underscores its importance'), negative parallelisms, vague attribution, and outline-like 'Challenges/Future Outlook' conclusions.
- Negative parallelism is the single most-cited sentence-level tell across sources, in three forms: 'not only X but also Y', 'It's not X—it's Y', and 'Y rather than X'. All three use negation-contrast scaffolding instead of a direct claim.
- A stable AI vocabulary list recurs across Wikipedia's guide, Plus AI's overused-ChatGPT-words analysis, and Embryo's list (updated March 2025): delve, landscape, robust, seamless, elevate, tapestry, testament, vibrant, pivotal, crucial, meticulous, intricate, foster, bolster, garner, showcase, underscore, realm, embark, leverage, boasts, interplay, burgeoning, plethora, multitude.
- The word 'delve' increased roughly 20-fold in academic papers in the first year after ChatGPT's release (Plus AI); research also found ChatGPT-overused words (delve, meticulous, boast, swift) subsequently increased in human speech — meaning the tell-list keeps contaminating human writing and must be checked by list, not vibe.
- AI avoids plain copulatives: 'is/are' gets replaced with 'serves as', 'stands as', 'functions as', 'represents', 'boasts', 'features', 'offers'. Wikipedia's guide flags this 'avoidance of copulatives' as a distinct grammatical signature.
- Formulaic openers ('In today's fast-paced world', 'In an ever-evolving landscape', 'In the digital age') and formulaic closers ('In summary', 'Overall', 'Ultimately', 'At the end of the day') are flagged by every source reviewed; AI defaults to essay structure — thesis opener, body, recap — even in web copy where nobody wants a recap.
- Rule-of-three stacking (triplet nouns: 'strategy, systems, and support'; triplet adjectives: 'fast, friendly, and reliable') is a recognized AI rhythm tell when it appears repeatedly; one triad per page is fine, triads in every paragraph is a signature.
- Em-dash overuse was a strong 2022-2025 tell and is still perceived as one by readers ('the ChatGPT dash'), but Wikipedia's guide now dates it as a historical/outdated signal for detection purposes — frequency matters more than presence; humans use em-dashes, just not three per paragraph.
- Hedging is a double tell: epistemic hedges ('can help', 'may improve', 'might be useful') drain conviction, and editorial hedges ('It's important to note', 'It is worth mentioning', 'Remember that') are canned AI phrases flagged by Wikipedia and Embryo.
- Vague attribution ('Experts argue', 'Studies show', 'Industry reports suggest', 'Many business owners find') without a nameable source is a Wikipedia-flagged AI pattern and is independently fatal to trust-based consulting copy.
- Detection research nuance: classic detectors (GPTZero et al.) used perplexity + burstiness, and humans do write with more varied sentence length; but a 2026 analysis (opace.agency, 5,935 matched document pairs) found sentence-length burstiness now scores AUROC 0.521 — near chance — against current models. Uniform rhythm is still a perceptual tell for readers even though it's no longer a reliable machine detector.
- What makes copy read human (Visual Communication Guy, May 2025; Pencil community, Aug 2025; multiple copywriter sources): varied sentence length with short sentences used for emphasis, a clear stance instead of hedged neutrality, specific concrete detail (numbers, names, places), occasional rule-breaking (fragments, starting with And/But), and tolerated mild imperfection — over-polished grammatical perfection reads less trustworthy than natural rough edges.
- Over-parallel heading sets (every H2 the same grammatical shape: 'Driving Growth', 'Building Trust', 'Creating Impact' — all gerunds, all two words) plus Title Case everywhere and bold-label vertical lists ('**Strategy:** ...') are Wikipedia-flagged structural tells; AI formats like a slide deck, humans format unevenly.
- Generic metaphor families are a tell: journey ('embark on your growth journey'), weaving ('tapestry of services'), navigation ('navigate the complexities'), landscape ('marketing landscape'), unlocking ('unlock your potential'), elevation ('elevate your brand'). Each is a stock metaphor no specific person would reach for.
- 'Elegant variation' — cycling synonyms to avoid repeating a word (clients → customers → patrons → clientele in one page) — is a listed AI signature; humans comfortably repeat the right word.
- Superficial-analysis verbs that assert meaning without evidence ('highlighting', 'underscoring', 'reflecting', 'fostering', 'ensuring', 'showcasing' as participial sentence tails: 'we run monthly campaigns, ensuring consistent growth') let a sentence claim significance it never demonstrates — Wikipedia flags the participial-tail construction specifically.
- For CPC specifically: the brand already bans hype and jargon, so the QA bar is higher — AI tells overlap heavily with the banned-word list (elevate, leverage, seamless are both AI tells and brand violations), and the strongest humanizers available are already in inventory: real prices ($1,200/$2,400/$4,800, $3,000), real constraints (8-client cap, 3-month minimum), real places (Indianapolis, central Indiana, neighborhood and street names), and named method steps (Conviction, Your people, A sharp offer, The experience, Word of mouth).

## Actionable
- 1. NEGATIVE PARALLELISM — cue: regex for "not just|not only|isn't just|more than just|It's not .*—it's|We don't .*\. We" — fix: state the positive claim directly with a specific. 'It's not just marketing—it's growth' → 'I help you get more customers. Here's how.' Max 0 per page.
- 2. AI VOCAB HITS — cue: match against ban list (delve, landscape, robust, seamless, elevate, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, meticulous, foster, bolster, showcase, underscore, leverage, holistic, empower, unlock, streamline, actionable) — fix: replace with the plain word (use→use, robust→solid/works, seamless→easy, elevate→improve) or cut. Max 0 per page.
- 3. FORMULAIC OPENERS — cue: page or section opens with 'In today's', 'In an era', 'In the world of', 'When it comes to', or any sentence true of every business on earth — fix: open with a specific claim, question, price, or scene ('Most Indianapolis trades businesses get their first ten customers from referrals. Then it stalls.'). Max 0.
- 4. SUMMARY ENDINGS — cue: final paragraph starts 'In summary/Overall/Ultimately/At the end of the day' or restates the section above — fix: end on the next step, a concrete detail, or just stop. Web pages end at the CTA, not a recap. Max 0.
- 5. SIGNIFICANCE INFLATION — cue: 'testament to', 'plays a [adj] role', 'stands/serves as', 'underscores', 'reflects a commitment to', 'cannot be overstated' — fix: show the evidence instead ('commitment to quality' → the 8-client cap, stated plainly). Max 0.
- 6. RULE-OF-THREE STACKING — cue: count triplet lists (A, B, and C) per page; flag >1, and flag any paragraph containing two — fix: cut to the one item that matters, or extend to an uneven 2 or 4; keep at most one deliberate triad per page.
- 7. VAGUE ATTRIBUTION — cue: 'experts', 'studies show', 'research suggests', 'many businesses', 'industry reports' with no named source — fix: name it, use first-person experience ('In eight years of doing this...'), or cut the claim. Max 0.
- 8. HEDGED NON-CLAIMS — cue: 'can help', 'may improve', 'might', 'could potentially', 'is designed to' — fix: commit ('This gets you more calls') or state the honest condition ('This works if you answer your phone'). Candid caveats are on-brand; mush is not.
- 9. CANNED EDITORIAL ASIDES — cue: 'It's important to note/remember', 'It is worth', 'Keep in mind', 'Remember that' — fix: delete the frame, keep the content; if it's important, it doesn't need announcing.
- 10. EM-DASH DENSITY — cue: >1 em-dash per ~150 words, or 2+ in one paragraph — fix: convert to periods, commas, or parentheses; keep em-dashes only where a real pause earns one.
- 11. UNIFORM SENTENCE RHYTHM — cue: compute sentence lengths per paragraph; flag paragraphs where every sentence is 15-25 words or all sentences share structure (subject-verb-object-clause) — fix: add a short sentence (≤6 words) after long ones; allow a fragment. Target: every page has at least a few sentences under 8 words.
- 12. COPULATIVE AVOIDANCE — cue: 'serves as', 'functions as', 'acts as', 'represents', 'boasts', 'features', 'offers' where 'is/has' would do — fix: use is/has. 'Carson serves as a strategic partner' → 'Carson is a strategist.'
- 13. GENERIC METAPHORS — cue: journey, navigate, unlock, roadmap-as-metaphor, tapestry, weave, landscape, elevate, next level, secret sauce — fix: either no metaphor, or one drawn from the client's actual world (trades, restaurants, nonprofits: 'a leaky pipeline' is earned for a plumber page, nowhere else).
- 14. OVER-PARALLEL HEADINGS — cue: 3+ consecutive H2/H3s with identical grammatical form (all gerunds, all 'Noun + Noun', all same word count) — fix: break the pattern; make at least one heading a plain statement or question ('What this costs', 'Who this isn't for').
- 15. TITLE CASE + FORMAT SOUP — cue: Title Case on every heading, bold-label lists ('**Strategy:** We...'), emoji bullets, 3+ bulleted lists per page — fix: sentence case headings; convert at least half the lists to prose; zero emoji in body copy.
- 16. ELEGANT VARIATION — cue: 3+ synonyms for the same referent on one page (clients/customers/patrons/partners; business/company/enterprise/organization) — fix: pick one word and repeat it. CPC default: 'clients' for CPC's own, 'customers' for the client's.
- 17. PARTICIPIAL SIGNIFICANCE TAILS — cue: sentences ending ', ensuring X', ', helping you Y', ', allowing Z', ', fostering W' — fix: make it its own sentence with evidence, or cut; the tail usually asserts a benefit the sentence didn't earn.
- 18. PROMOTIONAL ADJECTIVE CLUSTERS — cue: 2+ of vibrant/rich/renowned/comprehensive/innovative/cutting-edge/world-class/groundbreaking/diverse in a paragraph — fix: replace adjectives with one verifiable fact (number, name, date, constraint).
- 19. 'WHETHER X OR Y' FALSE RANGE — cue: 'Whether you're a startup founder or a family restaurant...' — fix: name the actual reader of this page; the site has dedicated pathways (local, founders, nonprofits), so each page addresses one of them directly.
- 20. MISSING SPECIFICITY (positive check) — cue: page contains zero numbers, zero place names, zero proper nouns beyond the brand — fix: every page gets at least two of: a real price, the 8-client cap, the 3-month minimum, an Indianapolis-area place name, a named method step, a day/timeframe ('you'll hear back within a day').
- 21. MISSING FIRST PERSON (positive check) — cue: page uses 'we' throughout or passive voice >10% of sentences, with no 'I' — fix: Carson is one person; default to 'I/you'; convert passives ('a plan is developed' → 'I write the plan'); at least one sentence of stated opinion per page ('I think most small businesses buy ads too early.').
- 22. OVER-POLISH / NO TEXTURE (positive check) — cue: zero contractions, zero sentences starting with And/But/So, zero fragments, zero asides — fix: add contractions everywhere natural; allow 1-2 conjunction-led sentences and one aside or admission per page ('This part is boring. Do it anyway.'). Mild imperfection reads as a person.
- 23. SAMENESS ACROSS PAGES (site-level check) — cue: diff openers and closers across all pages; flag any repeated opening construction or identical CTA phrasing on 3+ pages — fix: vary CTA wording per audience pathway while keeping the same destination.
- 24. READ-ALOUD GATE (final pass) — cue: any sentence you couldn't imagine Carson saying across a table in a Indianapolis coffee shop — fix: rewrite it as he'd say it, including the price out loud. If a sentence can't survive being spoken, it doesn't ship.

## Language bank
- BAN (vocab): delve, landscape, realm, robust, seamless, elevate, leverage, tapestry, testament, vibrant, pivotal, crucial, vital, meticulous, intricate, foster, cultivate, bolster, garner, showcase, underscore, embark, burgeoning, plethora, multitude, actionable insights, holistic, synergy, streamline, empower, unlock, supercharge, game-changing, skyrocket
- BAN (phrases): 'In today's fast-paced world', 'in an ever-evolving/changing landscape', 'In the digital age', 'It's important to note', 'It is worth mentioning', 'Remember that', 'At the end of the day', 'In summary', 'Overall,', 'Ultimately,', 'a testament to', 'plays a vital/pivotal/crucial role', 'stands as', 'serves as', 'navigate the complexities of', 'take your business to the next level', 'look no further', 'we've got you covered', 'whether you're X or Y'
- BAN (constructions): 'It's not just X—it's Y' / 'not only X but also Y' / 'We don't do X. We do Y.' (negative parallelism); participial tails ('...ensuring long-term growth', '...helping you thrive'); 'Experts/studies/many owners say...' with no name; three-item lists more than once per page
- USE (CPC humanizers): '$1,200 a month', '$3,000, one time', 'I only take eight clients at a time', 'three months minimum — growth work doesn't show up in week two', 'Indianapolis', 'central Indiana', 'Broad Ripple', 'Fountain Square', 'on the north side', 'a plumber in Greenwood', 'a taco place on Mass Ave', 'Conviction', 'Your people', 'A sharp offer', 'The experience', 'Word of mouth'
- USE (human registers): 'Here's the honest version:', 'I'll tell you if I'm the wrong fit', 'That's it. That's the whole pitch.', 'Most of this isn't complicated. It's just work.', 'I've seen this go wrong when...', 'You probably don't need X yet', 'And yes, that includes...', 'But here's the part nobody mentions', sentence fragments for emphasis ('Eight clients. That's the cap.')
- USE (concrete verbs instead of abstraction): run, build, write, fix, cut, call, ship, launch, answer, show up — not optimize, enhance, facilitate, streamline, empower, enable
