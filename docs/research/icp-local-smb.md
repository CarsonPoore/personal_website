# Research: icp-local-smb

## Summary
Central Indiana owner-operated SMBs ($250K-$5M: trades, home services, restaurants, retail, salons) enter 2026 squeezed: Indianapolis metro GDP growth is projected at only 1.5-2.0% with unemployment drifting above 4%, inflation is back as the #1 NFIB-reported problem, and consumer discretionary spending is cooling — so every marketing dollar is scrutinized. These owners are word-of-mouth businesses whose referral engine has stopped scaling; they search Google reactively (problem-first: "why am I not showing up on Google," "need more customers") more than category-first ("marketing company indianapolis"), and they carry deep, specific agency scar tissue: vague retainers, 12-month lock-ins, buzzword reports, and no connection between spend and booked jobs — 40% of outsourcing SMBs churn from their agency, 60% citing lack of ROI. They buy from people, not firms: a local, named human with plain pricing, no long contract, proof in the form of real local clients and real numbers (calls, booked jobs, revenue), and a willingness to say what WON'T work. Carson's published pricing ($1,200-$4,800/mo), 8-client cap, 3-month minimum, and candid-friend voice map almost perfectly onto this segment's objections — the pathway page's job is to say the quiet parts (what you get, what it costs, how you'll know it's working, how you leave) in the first screen.

## Key findings
- Agency churn is the dominant backdrop: a vcita survey of 500 US SMB owners (April 2025; home services, beauty, healthcare, business services) found 52% outsource marketing (spending up to $3,000/mo), 40% discontinue or switch agencies, 56% of that churn happens within 6-12 months, and 60% cite lack of ROI as the reason for leaving (vcita.com/press/smb-survey-highlights-marketing-agency-churn).
- The services cut first when SMBs fire an agency are social media management, paid search, and email — i.e., exactly the deliverables most agencies lead their pitch with. Lead with outcomes (booked jobs, full tables, repeat customers), not channel names (vcita survey; whatagraph.com/blog/articles/why-clients-fire-their-ad-agency).
- Poor communication/reporting is a firing offense independent of results: an UpCity survey found 80% of clients who leave agencies cite poor communication; 75% of marketing managers in a separate survey had dismissed an agency over poor reporting (whatagraph.com).
- The top problems SMBs actually want marketing help with are conversion and lead-gen, not branding: converting leads into paying customers (19.2%), generating new leads (16%), brand awareness (11.6%) (vcita survey).
- Documented burn stories in home services are extreme and shape the trade grapevine: a contractor paid $14,775 for website/SEO/social with almost no work done, and on cancellation the agency killed his hosting and Google Business Profile number, taking his phones down for two days; others paid $25K for websites that took 9 months while receiving 'screenshots of vanity metrics' (blitzmetrics.com). Expect prospects to arrive pre-burned.
- Contractors' stated red flags when vetting: 'we'll double your leads in 30 days' promises, cookie-cutter campaigns that never ask about service area or average job value, confusing reports + 'excuses about the algorithm,' inability to say where the money goes, and the pitch person disappearing after signing (hookagency.com/blog/vetting-a-marketing-agency).
- Proof contractors demand before hiring: calls/booked-jobs/revenue numbers (not impressions), case studies from their specific industry, direct conversations with current clients, clear timelines to results, and transparent monthly reporting (hookagency.com).
- Economic pressure, Indianapolis metro 2026: real GDP growth projected 1.5-2.0%, unemployment rising from 3.3% to just over 4.0%, with slowdowns in logistics and durable-goods manufacturing and 'pullbacks in consumer spending' named as drags — direct headwinds for restaurants, retail, and discretionary home projects (Indiana Business Research Center, incontext.indiana.edu/2026/jan-feb/article1.asp).
- NFIB 2026: inflation returned as the single most important problem (21% of owners by mid-2026, highest since Oct 2024), labor quality close behind, and a net negative 9% of owners reported higher nominal sales — owners feel squeezed on both costs and revenue, which makes 'no long contract, cancel-able, capped price' positioning land hard (nfib.com; tradingeconomics.com NFIB index).
- Tariffs are a named Indiana-specific pressure: materials costs (steel, aluminum, copper) up 30-50% for US buyers hit trades and remodelers directly; the Indiana SBA economic outlook and IBRC both flag tariffs + consumer pullback as 2026 themes (in.gov/sba; incontext.indiana.edu).
- Local search behavior is where these buyers live: ~46% of Google searches have local intent, 76% of 'near me' mobile searches lead to a physical visit within 24 hours, and businesses in the Google local 3-pack get 126% more traffic and 93% more actions than positions 4-10 (BrightLocal local SEO statistics, brightlocal.com/learn/local-seo-statistics).
- Reviews are the trust currency: GBP listings with 40+ reviews and a 4.5+ average appear in the local 3-pack ~3.7x more often than those with fewer than 10 reviews; Google holds ~81% of review volume. 'Get found + get reviewed' is a concrete, explainable early win Carson can promise honestly (BrightLocal; wiserreview.com).
- Spending norms validate the pricing page: 75%+ of small businesses spend $500-$3,000/mo on marketing and SBA guidance is 7-8% of gross revenue under $5M — so $1,200/mo is a normal commitment for a ~$300K business and $4,800/mo for a ~$1M+ business. Frame tiers as percent-of-revenue sanity checks, not arbitrary packages (diymarketers.com survey; clicksgeek.com budget guide).
- Buying triggers, in order of observed frequency: (1) phones/walk-ins slow down and word of mouth stops being enough; (2) a competitor visibly outranks them on Google or 'has a line out the door'; (3) revenue plateau despite working harder/DIY-ing more channels; (4) owner burnout — did the marketing themselves nights and weekends and can't anymore; (5) a bad agency or Angi/lead-platform experience they're recovering from (christopholivierconsulting.com fractional CMO triggers; gethearth.com; r/smallbusiness threads).
- Pay-per-lead platforms (Angi/HomeAdvisor) are a shared enemy in the trades: documented complaints of continued billing after cancellation and shared/low-quality leads (~$80/lead vs ~$45 via owned Google Ads in one comparison) — positioning against renting leads toward owning your own pipeline resonates (wsoctv.com Angi complaint; housecallpro.com; gethearth.com).
- Referrals from other owners are the #1 trusted channel for choosing providers: 71% of small business owners trust referrals from fellow owners; referral conversion runs ~4x other channels; marketing-service providers as a category score LOWER on trust than other SMB vendor categories — so Carson's site must read like a referral, not a pitch (fundbox.com SMB trust survey; Alignable SMB Trust Index; BNI data).
- Restaurant/salon/retail flavor of the pain is time and content, not leads vocabulary: 'maintaining social media is a full-time job,' accounts 'gathering dust,' September/post-holiday slow seasons, 'I post on social media and that's about it' — these owners buy done-for-you consistency and local visibility, not strategy decks (restaurantbusinessonline.com; kitomba.com; behindthechair.com; abcnews4.com).
- Reddit owner voice confirms the overcharge-not-scam pattern: 'I wasn't ripped off in the sense that I got nothing... more overcharged. Paid marketing agency $1k/m' — the typical wound is paying real money for invisible activity, not outright fraud. The antidote is radical legibility: show the work every month (r/smallbusiness thread 7m7o76 excerpt via search).
- AI is an opening, not a buzzword, for this audience when framed as labor relief: SMBs whose agencies lack AI capabilities want it specifically for creating content, auto-responding to inquiries (speed-to-lead), and generating estimates — maps directly to Carson's Technical practice (AI agents, GoHighLevel automations) if described in plain outcomes like 'every call and form gets answered in under a minute' (vcita survey).

## Actionable
- FIRST 100 WORDS of the local-business pathway page must do four things, in this order: (1) name their situation in their words — the work is good, word of mouth built the business, but the phone has gone quiet and the competitor down the road keeps showing up first on Google; (2) acknowledge the scar — they've probably paid someone before and couldn't tell what they got; (3) state the difference in one plain sentence — published prices, no long contracts (3-month minimum, then month to month), 8 clients total, one person who answers the phone; (4) one concrete outcome promise tied to booked work, e.g. 'when someone in Indianapolis searches for what you do, you show up — and when they call, someone answers.' Zero jargon; no 'funnel,' 'ROI-driven,' 'leverage,' 'growth engine.'
- TOP 5 OBJECTIONS TO PRE-EMPT ON THE PAGE: (1) 'I got burned before / how are you different' — answer with 3-month minimum then month-to-month, you own everything (your website, your Google profile, your ad accounts — leave anytime and keep it all), named references they can call; (2) 'I can't tell if marketing works' — promise a monthly plain-English report counting calls, booked jobs, and dollars, never impressions, plus call tracking from day one; (3) '$1,200-$4,800 is a lot right now' — frame against percent-of-revenue norms (most businesses your size spend $500-$3,000/mo already, often on Angi-type leads they rent instead of own) and against the cost of one empty crew-day or slow Friday night; (4) 'I don't have time for this' — state the owner's total time cost: one 30-minute call a month; (5) 'you won't understand my business' — show trades/restaurant/retail-specific proof and say plainly that the first thing that happens is Carson asking about average job value, service area, and best customers — the questions the last agency never asked.
- SERVICES TO LEAD WITH for this segment, in order: (1) Local visibility — Google Business Profile + reviews + local SEO ('show up when Indianapolis searches for what you do'), because it is the highest-trust, most explainable early win (3-pack businesses get 126% more traffic; 40+ reviews at 4.5+ stars = 3.7x more 3-pack appearances); (2) Speed-to-lead automation from the Technical practice, framed as 'every call, text, and form answered in minutes, even when you're on a roof' (GoHighLevel + AI agents, never named as such on the page); (3) Google Ads done honestly ('own your leads instead of renting them from Angi'); (4) done-for-you consistency (email to past customers, social presence) for restaurants/retail/salons. Do NOT lead with strategy, branding, or coaching for this pathway — hold the $3,000 strategy engagement as the step-up for owners who ask 'what should we do first,' and position retainers as the default entry.
- PROOF REQUIREMENTS this audience needs before contacting: at least one case study per vertical cluster (a trade, a restaurant/retail) with real dollar/call/booked-job numbers and a named local business; Google reviews of Carson's own practice prominently embedded (practice what's preached); the published pricing table itself is proof — keep it on the pathway page, not behind a 'book a call'; a photo and short plain bio of Carson (they are buying a person); an explicit 'what you keep if you leave' list; offer 'talk to a current client' as a vetting step. If a number can't be verified from a real client yet, use the industry stats above with attribution rather than inventing results.
- SEO/CONTENT TARGETING: build for two search modes. Category-first: 'marketing company indianapolis,' 'marketing agency for contractors indianapolis,' 'restaurant marketing indianapolis,' plus suburb variants (Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Noblesville, Avon, Brownsburg). Problem-first (higher intent, less competition): 'why is my business not showing up on google,' 'how to get more hvac/plumbing leads,' 'is angi worth it for contractors,' 'how much should a small business spend on marketing,' 'what to do when business slows down,' 'how to get more google reviews.' Each problem-first query deserves a candid, number-backed article in Carson's voice that ends at the pathway page.
- TONE CALIBRATION: every section should sound like the candid friend debriefing a bad agency experience — willing to say 'you might not need ads yet,' 'don't buy social media management if your Google profile is empty,' and 'here's what I'd do first if you spend nothing with me.' Naming what they should NOT buy is the single strongest differentiator against the overpromise pattern this audience has been trained to expect.
- TIMING/SEASONAL HOOKS for campaigns and page copy: Q1 (Jan-Mar) trades slow season and restaurant post-holiday dip are the highest-receptivity windows ('fix the pipeline before spring rush'); late summer back-to-school dip for restaurants/retail; frame 2026 economy honestly — costs up, consumers more careful — as the reason marketing has to be measured, not the reason to go dark.

## Page ideas
- Pathway page: /local-business (or /indianapolis-small-business) — the full dedicated pathway with first-100-words spec, objections, pricing table, vertical proof blocks.
- Vertical landing pages under the pathway: /for-contractors (trades & home services), /for-restaurants, /for-shops-and-salons (retail + beauty) — same skeleton, swapped pain language and proof.
- 'What it costs' / pricing page that keeps the $1,200/$2,400/$4,800 + $3,000 strategy numbers public and adds the percent-of-revenue sanity-check framing and 'what you keep if you leave' list.
- 'How to vet anyone (including me)' page or article: the 10 questions to ask any marketing company before signing — weaponizes the trust gap and earns problem-first search traffic.
- Problem-first article cluster: 'Why your business isn't showing up on Google,' 'Is Angi worth it for Indiana contractors?,' 'How much should a $500K business spend on marketing?,' 'The slow-season playbook for Indianapolis restaurants,' 'How to get 40+ Google reviews without begging.'
- 'What we'd do first' interactive or static mini-audit: owner answers 5 plain questions (what you do, service area, where leads come from now, what you've tried, what a good month looks like) and gets an honest first-90-days outline — feeds the $3,000 strategy engagement.
- Local proof page: Indianapolis/central-Indiana case studies with map of suburbs served (Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Noblesville) reinforcing local SEO and 'I'm from here' trust.
- FAQ section on the pathway page built directly from the objection list: contracts, cancellation, who does the work, how results are reported, 'do you work with my competitor?' (the 8-client cap answers exclusivity elegantly — one per trade per territory is worth stating).
