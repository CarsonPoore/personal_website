# Research: seo-national

## Summary
National SERPs for Carson's technical + coaching keywords split cleanly into two tiers: head terms ("fractional CMO," "AI automation consultant," "GoHighLevel expert") are owned by funded content machines (MarketerHire, Chief Outsiders, GoFractional) and marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr, Clutch) — not winnable directly — while question-format long-tails ("what does a fractional CMO cost," "is GoHighLevel worth it for small business," "how to use AI agents in a small business," "is a fractional CMO worth it for a small business") are currently ranked by solo consultants and small agencies with thin, listicle-grade content, which a candid first-person answer page can beat. The highest-leverage intersections are underserved: fractional CMO content aimed at sub-$1M local businesses (nearly all incumbents target Series A–B SaaS with $5–15K/mo retainers, leaving Carson's $1,200–$4,800 pricing as a differentiator worth publishing), and GoHighLevel + home-services content (missed-call text-back, review automation) which maps directly to his trades/home-services pathway. Recommended build: 4 national service pages (fractional CMO, AI agents & automation, GoHighLevel setup, analytics/GA4) each paired with 2–4 question-format articles that answer cost/worth-it/comparison queries with real numbers, structured for AEO (direct answer in first 2 sentences, FAQ schema, published pricing).

## Key findings
- Fractional CMO head-term SERP is dominated by marketplaces and funded content sites (MarketerHire, GoFractional, Chief Outsiders, Kalungi, marketful.com) — a solo consultancy cannot win 'fractional CMO' nationally, but the cost/worth-it long-tails are held by solo operators (matthewdeal.com, kamyarshah.com, automatethejourney.com, lilachbullock.com), proving single-practitioner sites DO rank for these queries.
- Published fractional CMO market rates: $3,000–$15,000/mo retainers, $150–$350/hr, 10–20 hrs/week per client (MarketerHire, Chief Outsiders, Kalungi, 2026 guides). Carson's $1,200–$4,800 retainers sit BELOW the published floor — a legitimate, citable price advantage for the small-business segment every incumbent ignores.
- Almost all ranking fractional CMO content targets Series A–B startups with $5M–$30M funding and 2–6 person marketing teams (MarketerHire, GoFractional). The 'fractional CMO for small business / local business owner' angle is held by only one or two thin pages (automatethejourney.com's 'Is a Fractional CMO Worth It for a Small Business?') — an open lane.
- A recurring objection in ranking 'worth it' content: fractional CMOs 'give you the plan but don't run the work,' a poor fit for small businesses needing strategy AND hands (automatethejourney.com). Carson's four-practice model (strategy + technical execution under one roof) directly answers the top objection in the SERP — this is the differentiating argument for his fractional CMO page.
- 'Fractional CMO vs marketing agency' is an established comparison query with 8+ dedicated ranking pages (lilachbullock.com, liftedlogic.com, upgrowth.in, pipelineroad.com); the consensus frame is 'CMO = clarity/strategy, agency = capacity/execution.' Carson can rank with a candid version and uniquely claim 'we're both' — but the vs-page format is table stakes.
- PAA-pattern questions around fractional CMO confirmed live in SERPs: 'what does a fractional CMO do,' 'how many hours does a fractional CMO work' (answer: 10–20 hrs/wk), 'when to hire a fractional CMO,' 'fractional CMO vs marketing consultant' (consultant advises, CMO owns outcomes), 'fractional CMO vs full-time CMO' ($175K–$300K+ full-time comp is the standard comparison anchor).
- 'AI automation consultant' / 'AI consulting for small business' SERP is immature: ranked by Clutch profiles, Upwork gigs, and small shops (bushe.co, valiantdigital.ca, kamyarshah.com) — far softer than the fractional CMO SERP. Published pricing: $150–$250/hr independents, $1,500–$6,000 defined projects, $800–$1,500/mo starter retainers (bushe.co, rapidevelopers.com, kamyarshah.com). This is Carson's most winnable national service-page keyword.
- A citable caution stat circulating in AI-consulting SERPs: 61% of small businesses saw no measurable ROI in year one, median engagement $28,000 (reported via rapidevelopers.com/kamyarshah.com — verify primary source before citing on-site, or paraphrase as 'most AI consulting engagements fail to pay off in year one'). A candid 'when NOT to hire an AI consultant' section matches both the SERP and Carson's no-hype voice.
- 'AI agents for small business' SERP is SaaS vendor blogs (botpress, nexos.ai, lowcode.agency) answering 'what are they' — nobody ranking owns the practitioner angle 'what an AI agent can actually do in a 5-person business and what it costs to set up.' Ranking content converges on the same use cases: inbox triage, lead qualification, follow-ups, scheduling, review requests — Carson should name these exact five.
- 'How to use AI agents in a small business' and variants are question queries answered today by generic listicles; the advice pattern ('start with one repeatable task, keep a human on final approval, build on tools you already use') is consistent and easy to beat with a concrete walkthrough from real client work.
- GoHighLevel SERP: 'GoHighLevel expert/setup' is Upwork/Fiverr/stealthagents territory ($15–$30/hr offshore to $1,600/mo dedicated), but 'is GoHighLevel worth it' and 'GoHighLevel vs HubSpot' rank small independent sites (automatethejourney.com, builts.ai, rsla.io, layer3labs.io) — winnable. Consensus answer: worth it for service businesses replacing 5–8 tools at $150+/mo, not worth it for solos under 50 leads; $97–$497/mo plus $30–$300 usage fees.
- GoHighLevel x home services is a proven content niche (ecosire.com, contractortoolstack.com, omnionlinestrategies.com rank for 'GoHighLevel for contractors/home services') with strong specifics: missed-call text-back as the #1 automation, 'three automations = 80% of the value' (instant SMS reply, appointment reminders, review requests), setup timeline 5–7 days to functional / 6–8 weeks to humming, GHL consultants charging $300–$5,000+ for setup. This bridges Carson's national technical practice and his local trades pathway.
- GA4 SERP: 'GA4 consultant' head term is agencies (analytify, mavlers, embryo) with published setup pricing $699–$2,000+ one-time — Carson can price-anchor against this. The volume is in problem queries: 'GA4 not tracking conversions,' 'Google Analytics not working,' 'why is my GA4 data wrong' — ranked by tool blogs (analytify.io, cometly.com), beatable with a diagnostic checklist article (events not marked as conversions, GTM tag placement, ad-blocker loss, attribution model confusion are the documented root causes).
- 'Marketing automation consultant' / 'CRM implementation small business' head terms have weak, fragmented SERPs (G2 discussion pages, partner directories, one Vendasta guide) — low volume but near-zero content competition; one solid service page can rank without supporting articles.
- 'Marketing advisor for founders' has no stable commercial SERP — it returns mentor marketplaces (GrowthMentor, MentorCruise, Clarity.fm) and individual Substacks. GrowthMentor's own ranking article admits the term 'fits almost anything and tells you almost nothing.' Do not build a page targeting this phrase; fold founder-advisory demand into the fractional CMO page and a 'marketing coach vs fractional CMO' article.
- Standard cost-comparison anchors to reuse site-wide (all verified in multiple ranking pages): full-time CMO $175K–$300K+; fractional = one-third to one-fifth the cost; fractional saves $150K–$250K/yr; GHL at 5,000 contacts ≈ $3,564/yr vs HubSpot ≈ $14,880/yr.
- AEO pattern across every topic researched: the pages winning featured-snippet position answer the question in the first two sentences with a number or a yes/no-with-conditions, then expand. Carson's published pricing and 8-client cap are exactly the kind of concrete, citable facts answer engines surface — put real numbers in the first paragraph of every money page.

- TITLE PATTERNS that match what's winning these SERPs: cost pages = '[Thing] Cost: Real Numbers for 2026' or 'What Does a [Thing] Cost?'; worth-it pages = 'Is [Thing] Worth It for a Small Business?'; comparisons = '[A] vs [B]: Which One Does Your Business Actually Need?'; how-tos = 'How to [Task] (Without [Common Failure])'; diagnostics = '[Problem]? Start Here.' Keep Carson's plainspoken voice in the subtitle, exact-match question in the H1/title tag.
- AEO REQUIREMENTS on every money page: answer in the first two sentences with a concrete number or conditional yes/no; FAQ schema with 3–5 PAA questions verbatim ('what does a fractional CMO do', 'how many hours does a fractional CMO work', 'is GoHighLevel worth it for a small business'); publish real pricing on service pages (answer engines cite pages with numbers; the 8-client cap and 3-month minimum are quotable differentiators); author byline + first-person experience signals (the solo sites outranking agencies on these queries all have them).
- DO NOT BUILD: a 'marketing advisor for founders' page (no stable SERP, marketplace-dominated); a page chasing bare 'fractional CMO' or 'GoHighLevel expert' head terms (marketplace/aggregator SERPs); any content repeating hype anchors like '100x growth' (violates voice and the SERP is saturated with it).
- INTERNAL LINKING: each article links only to its one matching service page; the fractional CMO service page links to the founders pathway page; the GoHighLevel article/page links to the local trades pathway — this wires the national technical/coaching content into the client-type pathways the site is being rebuilt around.
- STAT HYGIENE: ranges verified in ranking sources and safe to cite with attribution — fractional CMO $3K–$15K/mo and 10–20 hrs/wk; full-time CMO $175K–$300K+; GHL $97–$497/mo + usage; GA4 setup market $699–$2,000; AI projects $1,500–$6,000. The '61% no ROI / $28K median' AI stat appears in secondary sources only — paraphrase or trace to primary before publishing it as a number.
