Researched Oct 9, 2026 for Carson Poore Consulting. Every claim has a URL. "Not published" means no price found on pages read. Many pages are self-reported marketing; flagged where relevant.

## 2. Competitor profiles

### Lotiva (Lewisville, TX), AI automation with published prices
- URLs read: https://lotiva.com/pricing/ai-automation/ and https://lotiva.com
- Positioning: "AI-powered" marketing, SEO and design agency that added AI automation. Small and mid-sized businesses, nationwide.
- Pricing (published, rare): Starter from $1,500 (one workflow), Growth from $5,000 (AI assistant trained on your materials, connected to CRM/calendar), Premium from $12,500 (multiple automations, monthly hours-saved reports), Enterprise custom. Monitoring $150/mo, support $150/hr, extra automations from $1,500. AI usage paid direct to provider, not marked up. Clients own everything built.
- Proof: 5.0 on Google (22 reviews per home page); testimonials are mostly marketing results, not automation. Headline numbers (5x ROAS, $350M managed spend, 8M leads, 47+ industries) are self-reported, no method.
- AI risk: strongest of the set. Answers only from client materials, sensitive items can require human approval, hands off when unsure, edge-case testing before launch. Home page also says AI cannot replace strategy and judgment, yet uses "even while you sleep" language.
- Content/SEO: commercial city and niche posts (med spa, best agencies in Miami), answer-first openings, free SEO audit lead magnet.

### Omni Online Strategies (Davie, FL), GHL build agency
- URLs: https://omnionlinestrategies.com/gohighlevel-agency and the pricing blog above.
- Positioning: "We build your entire GHL stack"; B2B, healthcare, construction, legal, real estate, coaches, white-label agencies.
- Pricing: not published, scoped quote. Claims 2-3 week setup, A2P 10DLC included.
- Proof: no named clients or case studies. Unsourced claim that most GHL setups fail because SMS gets filtered.
- AI: Conversation AI on OpenAI, "escalates to a human when needed", no accuracy testing described.
- Content: year-stamped "GoHighLevel pricing" article, worked 10-client example, referral-tagged GHL links with no on-page disclosure I could see. Unverified "60,000 agencies", G2 4.4 claims; "60 to 80 percent cheaper than HubSpot" is unsupported.

## 3. Patterns across the set

- Pricing: only Lotiva, AutogenCRM and Growthable publish real numbers. Most hide behind "book a call". None publishes monthly retainer tiers with a stated minimum term, a client cap, or a nonprofit rate. CPC's published $1,200/$2,400/$4,800 retainers and $3,000 strategy are unusual for this category.
- Cheap-setup end: $197-$997 one-time (AutogenCRM), $299-$997 (Growthable). Mid: $1,500+ (Lotiva). Enterprise-ish: $8,000-$75,000 (Layer3's generic guide).
- Proof is thin. Almost no named clients, measured outcomes or methods. Self-assigned "Top Rated" badges are common.
- SEO approach: year-stamped "pricing" and "review" articles, comparison pages, trial funnels, many monetized through HighLevel referral links; disclosure quality varies (Net Partners, AutogenCRM disclose; Omni and SupplyGem less clearly).
- AI risk: most pitch autonomy. Human-in-the-loop language appears at Lotiva (best), Omni ("escalates when needed"), and generically in Layer3's guide. GrowwStacks has none and even suggests background audio so the AI sounds like an office.

## 4. Hype claims to avoid or counter

- "24/7 while you sleep", "sounds incredibly human", "predictable results" (GrowwStacks, Lotiva).
- Profit-per-call math with no call-length, booking-rate or churn data (GrowwStacks).
- "Top Rated", "best reviews", "most recommended" with no source (AutogenCRM, Stealth Agents).
- "Save 60-80%" vs hiring or vs HubSpot, no method (Stealth Agents, Omni).
- "Most setups fail because SMS gets filtered" (Omni, unsourced; though A2P 10DLC registration is a real requirement).
- Stale "2026" pricing posts with old plan names and old AI rates (SupplyGem, GrowwStacks).
- Setup timelines that contradict themselves (AutogenCRM 5 days vs 1-2 weeks).

## 5. The honest-positioning gap CPC can own

1. A real cost-of-ownership page: HighLevel plan + usage (SMS, voice minutes, tokens, A2P fees) in plain numbers, with last-verified dates, and the client owns the account.
2. Human-in-the-loop as a written spec: what the AI may say, what needs approval, what escalates, who reviews logs weekly. Only Lotiva comes close.
3. No lock-in and no resale mark-up: CPC does not need to sell HighLevel as its own SaaS. Say so, and say whether CPC earns any referral commission.
4. Strategy before software: the $3,000 engagement decides whether GHL or AI is needed at all. Competitors all sell the tool first.
5. Plain pricing with a cap: $1,200/$2,400/$4,800, 3-month minimum, 8 clients.
