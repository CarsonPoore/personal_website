# Competitor Set E: Nonprofit marketing and Google Ad Grants management

Researched Oct 9, 2026. Every claim is cited. "Not published" means the competitor's own pages did not state it. Competitor proof figures are self-reported marketing claims, not verified.

## 1. Google's current Ad Grants rules (for CPC copy accuracy)

Verified on Google's own help pages (support.google.com/nonprofits) unless noted.

| Item | What Google says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Amount | Up to $10,000 USD per month of in-kind text search advertising on Google Search. Roughly $329/day. It is a limit, not a guarantee of spend. No spend requirement, unused funds do not roll over. Grant never covers overspend. | https://www.google.com/nonprofits/find-solutions/ad-grants/ ; https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166?hl=en ; https://www.google.com/grants/faq/ |
| CTR | Accounts not exclusively on Smart Campaigns must keep a 5% CTR each month, measured at the account level, not per keyword. Missing it two consecutive months can cause temporary deactivation. Reactivation needs fixes plus a reactivation request. | https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en ; https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/117827?hl=en |
| Quality Score | Keywords with a Quality Score of 1 or 2 must be paused or removed. Keywords showing "-" are exempt. Single-word keywords (outside own brand terms and some exceptions) and overly generic keywords are not allowed. | https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en |
| Structure | At least 2 ad groups per campaign, tightly themed, and at least 2 sitelink assets. Proper geo-targeting. | same |
| Conversion tracking | Accounts created since Jan 1, 2018, or using Smart Bidding, must track valid conversions and record at least 1 conversion per month. Accounts created on or after April 22, 2019 must have a qualifying conversion type. Soft metrics (time on site, homepage visit) go in "Other," not "Conversions." | https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/117827?hl=en |
| Bidding | Current Google guidance recommends Maximize conversion values (if tracking donations, purchases, fees) or Maximize conversions. Maximize clicks is a starting point until about 15-30 conversions in 30 days. Google's current bidding page does not state a $2 max CPC cap. The $2 cap appears only in older material, so do not cite it. | https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/1332166?hl=en |
| Annual survey | Annual program survey is required. Non-participation can lead to deactivation. | https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/9314402?hl=en |
| Website policy | Nonprofit must own the domain, no redirect to a third-party or social page, substantial unique mission content, visible mission, address and/or EIN, working navigation and donation buttons, fast, mobile-friendly, HTTPS site-wide, no AdSense/excess third-party ads. | https://support.google.com/grants/answer/1657899 |
| Enforcement | Accounts in violation are subject to automatic suspension without notification. | https://support.google.com/nonprofits/answer/46103?hl=en |
| Eligibility | Must be an eligible nonprofit. Agency Elevation states government entities, hospitals and schools are generally excluded (Elevation, not Google, wording): https://www.elevationweb.org/google-ad-grant/ . Check Google's eligibility page before writing exclusions into CPC copy. | |
| Agencies | Google promotes "Certified Professional Agencies" and cites a "8x" higher conversion value for managed grantees. | https://www.google.com/nonprofits/find-solutions/ad-grants/ |

Not found on Google's pages: any "log in every 30 days / make changes every 90 days" rule. Older agency content repeats this. I found no current Google source for it, so CPC copy should say "keep the account active and compliant" rather than quote a login cadence. Google's pages mention "Performance Max" and other campaign types only via third parties (a BrightonSEO 2026 talk description and agency blogs), so do not claim specifics about PMax or AI Max eligibility in grants without checking inside an account.

Wording tip: "up to $10,000 a month" (Google's phrase), not "$10,000 a month guaranteed." Several agencies say "$10K/$11K" or "free money"; Google calls it in-kind search advertising.

### Market pattern
- Published monthly fees cluster at $350-$600 for management only; $1,200-$1,750 where content or paid ads are bundled; success-fee model of 5-7% of spend exists (AboveX). Source for range: REACHRIGHT guide and the first-party pages above.
- Setup fees: from none to $2,000 (REACHRIGHT, waived with 12 months).
- Contracts: month-to-month is common (Digital Tabby, AboveX after 3 months); 3-month minimums exist (REACHRIGHT-cited Blue Tusk, RankMonsters).
- Most firms do not publish prices. Getting Attention, Project World Impact, and REACHRIGHT are the exceptions among those read first-party.
- No competitor read first-party advertised a reduced nonprofit rate for management. Charitable Allies is the only Indiana-relevant firm with an explicit discounted-rate model, in legal services.
- Proof is almost always self-reported counts of nonprofits, utilization, or CTR.

## 4. Opportunities (what the research implies)
- Local gap: no Indiana Ad Grants specialist found. CPC can claim "Indianapolis-based" but must not claim "only" or "first" without checking Google's Certified Professional Agency directory.
- Price transparency is rare. Publishing a fixed Ad Grants management price puts CPC beside Getting Attention, Project World Impact and REACHRIGHT, but ahead of most.
- Contract fairness: month-to-month after a short minimum matches the best-priced rivals. CPC's 3-month minimum equals Blue Tusk and RankMonsters (per secondary source).
- Competitors rarely offer reduced nonprofit rates. CPC's nonprofit rate is a differentiator if it is stated plainly with a number.
- Few Ad Grants specialists bundle website readiness (Google's website policy), brand, video and local content. CPC's four practices can fix the site problems that cause denials.
- Accuracy as positioning: Google's rules are specific (5% CTR, QS, 2 ad groups, 2 sitelinks, conversion tracking). A plain-English compliance explainer on carsonpoore.com would be rare and linkable.
