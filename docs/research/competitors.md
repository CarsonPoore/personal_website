# Research: competitors

## Summary
Across 13 competitor sites read (6 Indianapolis agencies, 4 fractional-CMO/outsourced-CMO practices, 3 AI-automation shops), only one — OptiWork.ai — publishes real pricing; everyone else gates cost behind a "free consultation." Indianapolis agencies either chase enterprise/B2B verticals (Element Three, TrendyMinds, Hirons) or publish template-driven local-SEO pages with no local substance (Emulent, Thrive's city pages), leaving the owner-led local small business with no candid, transparently priced, strategy-first option. Fractional-CMO firms lean on trademarked frameworks (Growth Gears®, Authentic Growth®, Random Acts of Marketing®) and opaque $3k–$15k/mo retainers, and AI-automation shops either skew enterprise or lack proof. Nobody combines local Indianapolis presence + transparent published retainers + technical build capability (AI/automation/CRM) + fractional-CMO coaching under one human voice — that intersection is empty and Carson can own it.

## Key findings
- Element Three (Indianapolis, elementthree.com) — 'The B2B2X Marketing Agency': pivoted entirely to manufacturers/OEM-dealer networks (RV, marine, powersports). No pricing, no small-business lane at all. Gap: Indy's most prominent agency has formally exited the local-SMB market.
- StrataBlue (Indianapolis, stratablue.com) — 'Fortune Favors the BRAVE™': sells 'AI-enabled operating systems' for revenue/service/HR. No pricing; jargon-heavy ('AI Opportunity Map', 'governance guardrails'); case-study metrics render as literal '0'. Gap: the local AI-automation claim exists but is enterprise-toned, hype-forward, and proof-broken.
- TrendyMinds (Indianapolis, trendyminds.com) — 'All impact. No friction.' / 'Agency of Preference®': targets enterprise B2B (Workday, Salesforce, Humana). No pricing, no homepage case studies or metrics, trademarked model jargon. Gap: inaccessible to anyone under mid-market.
- Willow Marketing (Indianapolis, willowmarketing.com) — 'Smart Strategy. Strong Creative. Real Results.': owns the associations/credentialing-bodies niche with some nonprofit work. No pricing, vague deliverables, thin social proof. Gap: 'nonprofit' positioning is really member-association positioning; small mission-driven orgs with real budgets are underserved and no one offers a stated nonprofit rate.
- Hirons (Indianapolis, hirons.com) — 'Bold Owners. Bold Ideas,' 48 years: government/healthcare/nonprofit verticals, case studies shown as logo tiles with no outcomes, generic testimonials, classic big-agency jargon ('integrated branding'). No pricing. Gap: legacy credibility without candor or specificity.
- Emulent and Thrive Internet Marketing both rank for 'marketing agency indianapolis' via template city pages — Emulent's Indianapolis page contains zero Indianapolis-specific content (their offices are in North Carolina) and identical service blocks repeated per vertical. Gap: the local-SEO SERP is won by non-local template farms; a genuinely local site with real Indy substance can outrank on relevance and reviews.
- Chief Outsiders (national fractional-CMO firm, chiefoutsiders.com) — 'Results, not Consulting' / 'Your On-Demand Growth Engine': 300+ PE firms, Growth Gears® proprietary framework, no pricing ('schedule a consultation' gatekeeping), case studies without quantified metrics. Gap: corporate, framework-branded, built for PE-backed mid-market — not owners.
- Authentic Brand (authenticbrand.com) — 'Overcome Random Acts of Marketing®': fractional CMO + Authentic Growth® methodology + peer community for founder-led SMBs. No pricing; heavy trademark soup; content-dense homepage with competing value props. Closest national analog to Carson's coaching practice, and still opaque on cost.
- Hawke Media (hawkemedia.com) — 'Your Outsourced CMO®': 22+ a-la-carte services, 6,000+ brands, awards-heavy. Claims modular flexibility but publishes no prices; homepage stats render as '$0 Billion' placeholders. Gap: 'outsourced CMO' at volume means no actual senior attention — the anti-8-client-cap.
- CMOx / Casey Stanton (cmox.co) — 'Solve Bigger Problems': splits audience between business owners and marketers wanting to BECOME fractional CMOs; admits the market range ($3,000–$15,000/mo) in content but publishes no rate card; cluttered with pop-ups and lead magnets. Gap: founder-brand fractional CMO sites monetize the category more than the client.
- Fractional-CMO market pricing (multiple 2026 guides: MarketerHire, Kamyar Shah, GoFractional, Clicks Geek): retainers run $3,000–$15,000/mo, averaging $10k–12k/mo; advisory-only tiers start ~$3,000–$4,000/mo at 10–15 hrs. Carson's $1,200–$4,800 retainers and $3,000 strategy engagement sit at or below the published floor of the national market — publishing them is a weapon, not a concession.
- OptiWork.ai (AI automation for owner-led SMBs) — 'Get Your Week Back From Repetitive Work': the ONLY site in all three sets with fully published pricing ($4,500 AI roadmap, automations from $12k, agents from $20k, $500/mo support). Candid anti-hype voice ('We don't sell hype'), human-in-the-loop framing. Weakness: zero testimonials or demonstrated results. Proves the transparent-pricing + plainspoken playbook works in AI services — and that their build-from-$12k floor leaves the sub-$5k SMB automation buyer unserved.
- Layer3Labs (layer3labs.io) — 'Independent AI benchmarks and deployment': strong vertical page architecture (9 industries incl. home services, construction, hotels — Carson's trades audience), free workflow audit, ROI calculators. No pricing, no case studies with outcomes. Gap: good IA, no local presence, no proof.
- Morningside AI (morningside.ai) — 'We don't just talk AI. We deliver it.': anti-hype positioning with Identify→Develop→Adopt process, but enterprise clients (Milwaukee Bucks), no pricing, no quantified case-study outcomes, FAQs that dodge hard questions. Gap: the plainspoken-AI voice exists but nobody pairs it with SMB price points.
- Cross-cutting pattern 1 — pricing opacity is near-universal: 12 of 13 sites reviewed publish no pricing and funnel to a consult/strategy-session CTA. Several guides explicitly note this 'makes comparison shopping impossible' — the friction Carson's published $1,200/$2,400/$4,800 + $3,000 tiers eliminate.
- Cross-cutting pattern 2 — broken proof is epidemic: three sites (StrataBlue, Element Three, Hawke) ship homepages with literal '0' / '$0 Billion' placeholder metrics; others show logo walls with no outcomes. Real numbers, named clients, and specific results would clear the bar almost alone.
- Cross-cutting pattern 3 — trademarked framework inflation: BRAVE™, Agency of Preference®, Growth Gears®, Authentic Growth®, Random Acts of Marketing®, 'Your Outsourced CMO®'. Carson's unregistered, plain-English five steps (Conviction → Your people → A sharp offer → The experience → Word of mouth) reads as the candid counter-position.
- Cross-cutting pattern 4 — no one bridges marketing and technical: Indy marketing agencies don't build AI/automation/CRM; AI shops don't do brand/strategy/coaching; fractional CMOs advise but don't implement. The one-team-four-practices claim (strategy + marketing + technical + coaching) has no direct competitor in any of the three sets.

## Actionable
- Publish all pricing on a top-nav Pricing page — $1,200/$2,400/$4,800 retainers, $3,000 strategy engagement, nonprofit rate — and say plainly 'most firms make you book a call to hear a number; here are ours.' (Counter: 12 of 13 competitors reviewed hide pricing behind a consultation CTA.)
- State the 8-client cap and 3-month minimum as a headline promise, framed as 'you get a person, not a pod' — the direct opposite of Hawke Media's 6,000-brands volume model and Chief Outsiders' bench of fractional execs.
- Make the one-team-four-practices claim explicit on the homepage: strategy, marketing, technical (AI agents, websites, GoHighLevel/CRM, Google Ads, GA4), and coaching from the same person — because Indy marketing agencies can't build, AI shops can't do brand, and fractional CMOs don't implement.
- Own 'actually in Indianapolis': real neighborhoods, real local clients, real photos. The 'marketing agency indianapolis' SERP is held by North Carolina template farms (Emulent) and national chains (Thrive) with zero local substance — authentic local pages beat them on relevance; pair with a complete Google Business Profile and local reviews.
- Write every page in the candid-friend voice and name the absence of hype as a feature — no trademarked framework, no ™/® methodology soup (BRAVE™, Growth Gears®, Authentic Growth® are the competitor tell). Present the five steps in plain English as 'how we work,' not a branded system.
- Show real proof with real numbers: named clients, specific outcomes, dated results. Three competitors literally display '0' or '$0 Billion' placeholder stats; most show logo walls. Even 3–5 honest case studies with one concrete number each clears the local bar.
- Build the three client pathways as dedicated pages (local small businesses/trades & home services, founders & startups, nonprofits & mission-driven) with pathway-specific proof and the nonprofit rate stated on the nonprofit page — only Willow serves nonprofits locally and even they hide price and skew toward large associations.
- Position AI & automation for the sub-$5k buyer in plain language with human-in-the-loop framing: OptiWork (the only transparent-pricing competitor) starts builds at $12,000, and Morningside/StrataBlue skew enterprise — 'practical automation at a small-business price' is open.
- Table stakes the site must not miss: free initial-conversation CTA on every page (universal pattern), a real services/practices nav with individual pages, visible founder bio and face (solo trust driver), case-study page, FAQ that answers hard questions directly (Morningside's evasive FAQ is the anti-pattern), mobile-fast pages, and vertical/industry landing pages for trades and home services (Layer3Labs' 9-industry IA is the best structural model seen).

## Language bank
- 'We don't sell hype' / 'We don't demo magic' — the anti-hype register already converting in AI services (OptiWork, StrataBlue); Carson's version should be warmer and less defensive
- 'Get your week back' — owner-pain time framing (OptiWork) that lands with trades/home-services owners better than any marketing metric
- 'Random acts of marketing' — the problem phrase Authentic Brand trademarked; the underlying owner feeling ('we post stuff and nothing happens') is free to describe in plain words
- 'You get the resources of a full-service agency with the attention of an in-house team' — the promise Hawke makes and (at 6,000 brands) can't keep; Carson's 8-client cap makes it true
- 'How much does a fractional CMO cost' — a high-intent query every national player answers with a blog post instead of a rate card; buyers searching it see $3,000–$15,000/mo ranges
- 'No long-term contracts' vs. Carson's honest inversion: 'three months minimum, because real change takes that long'
- Buyer-frustration phrases from the research: 'makes comparison shopping impossible', 'tools sit unused', 'endless pilots', 'book a call to hear a number'
- Owner questions competitors dodge: 'What exactly do I get each month?', 'Who actually does the work?', 'What happens if it doesn't work?'
