# Research: lead-magnets

## Summary
Interactive, instantly-scored self-assessments are the dominant high-converting lead magnet format for consultants in 2026, and the ungated constraint is an advantage, not a handicap: B2B content strategy has shifted to hybrid/ungated models because forms in front of PDFs capture curiosity, not intent, while instant-result tools build trust and qualify the visitor in the same five minutes. Every winning example studied (TechSoup's nonprofit marketing grader, Scorchsoft's Startup Readiness Score, Allied VC's Founder-Market Fit Scorecard, the free Google Business Profile audit tools, ScoreApp-style scorecards) follows the same spine: short question set, a numeric score with a named stage or grade, per-category breakdown showing the weakest area, and a next step framed as "talk through your results." All five recommended magnets below run as pure client-side HTML/JS (scoring logic, sliders, localStorage) with zero backend, and each one ends by naming a specific gap only a conversation can close.

## Key findings
- Scored self-assessments ('scorecard marketing', popularized by ScoreApp/Daniel Priestley) are the consultant lead-gen format with the most proof behind it: ScoreApp claims 5M+ leads across 151k businesses and publishes case quotes like 'cost per qualified lead dropped by half in the first month' after swapping ad landing pages for a scorecard (scoreapp.com/scorecard-marketing).
- The winning scorecard spine is consistent across every example read: ~10 questions, 2-3 minutes, overall score 0-100, a named stage/label, per-pillar sub-scores, and a 'weakest area' callout that creates the reason to talk. Scorchsoft's Startup Readiness Score (startuptools.scorchsoft.com) does exactly this: 5 pillars, 'Strong / Needs Work / Critical Gap' labels, 'Almost Ready' stage names, and comparative framing ('average founder scores 62, top 10% score 85+').
- Ungated is the 2026 default, not a compromise: multiple sources (Brixon Group's data-driven gating guide, Force24, GrowthSpree) report the shift to hybrid/ungated strategies because a form in front of a PDF 'signals mild curiosity, not buying intent' and gating throttles the reach that builds demand. Instant-result tools are repeatedly cited as outperforming static downloads (vida.io claims 2-3x; treat as a vendor figure, directionally consistent across sources).
- Trust signals matter on the tool itself: the highest-performing ungated assessments all state 'free', 'no credit card', 'no email required', '100% private', and 'results in 60 seconds' right on the start screen (Scorchsoft, GLocal GBP audit, Automate the Journey). For an ungated site these claims are true and should be said loudly — they are a differentiator against gated competitors.
- Free Google Business Profile audit tools are a proven, crowded-but-validated pattern for local SMBs: Slerp (5-category score for restaurants), GLocal/Wiremo (pass/fail checks, overall score, under a minute), FixAEO (16-point completeness scorecard with concrete fixes per item), Automate the Journey (8-point checklist, results in 15 seconds), GBP Auditor (letter grade). All are ungated, all monetize via a follow-up service conversation. A self-audit checklist version needs no API and works as pure HTML/JS.
- Nonprofit marketing self-assessments are an established category with two strong reference models: TechSoup's Digital Marketing Assessment (ungated grade-based quiz covering goals, content, email, social, automation, UX) and Mighty Citizen's 'Mighty GPS' (6-category marketing-maturity score with a named stage plus annual peer benchmarks). The benchmark/peer-comparison angle ('see how you compare to other nonprofits') is the hook that makes nonprofit leaders complete it.
- For founders, the credible assessments score readiness across named pillars rather than grading 'marketing': Scorchsoft scores Product-Market Clarity, Technical, Resources, Brand, Go-to-Market; Allied VC's Founder-Market Fit Scorecard (ungated, runs as a Claude artifact) gives a percentage plus investor-lens narrative feedback. Founders respond to 'readiness' and 'gaps' framing, not 'audit' framing.
- AI-readiness quizzes are the fastest-growing assessment subgenre and are directly relevant to the Technical practice: The Access Group runs a 10-question AI readiness quiz returning a stage + guidance; Hunted.space's 2-minute quiz tells you 'what to build first: agents, automation, or simpler tools'; Godberry Studios' 10-question version returns a readiness score plus an estimate of hours/dollars AI could save. The 'what should you automate first' verdict is the deliverable people actually want.
- Marketing-budget benchmarks are well-sourced enough to power an honest calculator: Gartner's 2025 CMO Spend Survey puts average marketing spend at 7.7% of revenue (enterprise-skewed); the SBA recommends 7-8% for companies under $5M revenue; stage-based guidance converges on roughly 10-20% early-stage, 7-10% growth, 4-7% mature (mercury.com, truefuturemedia.com, hawksem.com). A calculator can cite these ranges without fabricating anything.
- Calculators convert because they give a hyper-personalized number for minimal effort (vida.io, calconic): the proven consulting variants are 'calculate your marketing budget' (revenue + goals in, channel/budget breakdown out) and ROI/value calculators. The output number is also a pricing pre-frame: showing a visitor that 7-8% of their revenue is $X/month makes a $1,200-$4,800 retainer legible.
- Checklists remain the highest-ROI static-ish format for stressed service-business owners because they move someone from 'vague problem' to 'clear list of things to do' (vida.io); for contractors specifically, the formats that generate sales conversations are checklists, cost guides, comparison sheets, and free assessments/audits (clicksgeek.com). An interactive checklist (checkboxes + live score + localStorage) beats a PDF on an ungated site.
- The handoff pattern that works is never 'book a call' as a bare button — it is tied to the result: ScoreApp segments results into ready-to-buy / fit-for-offer / needs-nurture and routes follow-up accordingly; Mighty Citizen pairs the score with 'contact us' and the benchmark report; Free Range Future (nonprofit agency) wraps its 5-minute assessment in a productized 'marketing audit sprint' — the free self-score is explicitly the top of a paid-audit funnel.
- Several of the best tools in this space are themselves AI-era proof-of-craft: Allied VC built its scorecard as a Claude artifact; Hunted.space's quiz uses AI to analyze answers. For a consultancy selling AI agents & automation, the lead magnets themselves are portfolio evidence — a sharp interactive tool demonstrates the Technical practice better than any case study paragraph.
- Question-count sweet spot across all examples: 8-12 questions, 2-3 minutes, one question per screen, progress indicator. Longer assessments (Mighty GPS) justify themselves with benchmarks; short ones (Automate the Journey's 8-point, 15-second audit) win on frictionlessness. For ungated with no email capture, shorter wins — the goal is the result screen, not the data.
- Peer/comparative framing is the most repeated engagement device: 'average founder score 62', nonprofit benchmarks 'compiled from organizations that take the assessment', letter grades, '2 critical gaps found'. Even without a backend, honest static comparisons ('most owner-run businesses we assess score lowest on follow-up') create the same pull — but must be worded as qualitative observation, not fabricated data.

## Actionable
- BUILD #1 — 'The Five-Step Check-Up' (marketing system self-score quiz). Format: 10 questions, one per screen, 2 per method step (Conviction, Your people, A sharp offer, The experience, Word of mouth), each answered on a 1-4 'that's us / not yet' scale; pure JS scoring, radar-free bar breakdown, overall score 0-100 with a plainspoken stage name ('Solid footing', 'One weak link', 'Starting fresh'). Include a first-screen toggle: business / nonprofit (wording swaps only). Target ICP: all three; it is the site's flagship magnet and teaches the five-step method while scoring it. The one insight: which of the five steps is your weakest — the single thing dragging the rest down. Handoff: result screen names the weak step in plain words ('Your offer isn't sharp enough for word of mouth to work with') and the CTA is 'Bring your score to a free call — we'll spend 30 minutes on your weakest step.' Booking link carries the result as a URL param so the call starts pre-briefed.
- BUILD #2 — 'Can Indianapolis Find You?' (local visibility self-audit checklist). Format: 16-18 interactive check items in four groups (Google Business Profile completeness, reviews & replies, website basics/NAP, being-found extras like photos and local keywords), modeled on FixAEO's 16-point scorecard and Automate the Journey's 8-point audit; each item has a 'how to check this in 2 minutes' expander and a 'fix it yourself' note; live score updates as boxes tick, saved in localStorage so they can return. Target ICP: local SMBs — trades, home services, restaurants, retail in central Indiana. The one insight: a letter-grade-style visibility score plus the 3 highest-impact unchecked items. Handoff: 'Checked everything and still not showing up — or don't want to do the fixes yourself? That's a 30-minute call. We'll look at your listing together, live.' This page doubles as the local-SEO SEO page (Indianapolis keywords live here naturally).
- BUILD #3 — 'The AI Opportunity Finder' (automation picker). Format: visitor picks their business type (trades/restaurant/retail/professional services/nonprofit/startup), then checks off tasks they still do by hand from a curated list (chasing quotes, answering the same questions, follow-up texts, review requests, rekeying data between tools, scheduling); for each, they set their own 'hours per week' slider. Output: their tasks ranked easiest-to-automate first, their own hours total played back ('that's ~X hours a month, by your numbers' — no fabricated savings claims), and a plain-English note on what the fix looks like (an agent, an automation, or just a better-connected CRM) — the Access Group / Hunted.space 'what to build first' verdict pattern. Target ICP: local SMBs and founders; feeds the Technical practice. The one insight: your single best first automation, named. Handoff: 'The build is usually simpler than people expect. Book a call and we'll scope your first one in 30 minutes — you'll leave with the plan either way.'
- BUILD #4 — 'What Should Marketing Cost?' (budget calculator). Format: two inputs — annual revenue (slider/field) and stage (getting started / growing / steady) — returns a sourced monthly budget range: SBA's 7-8% for under-$5M businesses, stage bands of roughly 10-20% early / 7-10% growth / 4-7% mature, with Gartner's 7.7% average as context; cite all three on-page, show the math. Then it quietly maps the range onto the published retainers: 'For a business doing $400k, a healthy range is $2,300-$3,300/month — our $2,400 tier sits inside it.' Nonprofit toggle applies the reduced-rate note. Target ICP: local SMBs and founders in the pricing-anxiety stage of research. The one insight: a defensible dollar range for their actual revenue, ending the 'am I being ripped off?' spiral. Handoff: 'The number is the easy part — where it goes is the work. A free call gets you a first-pass allocation for your range.' This page also pre-frames pricing so discovery calls skip sticker shock.
- BUILD #5 — '90-Day Plan Starter' (interactive one-page plan builder). Format: a guided form — one goal, the one audience it serves, the offer, the two channels you'll actually keep up with, a weekly rhythm — that assembles a clean printable one-page 90-day plan (window.print() to PDF, copy-to-clipboard; no backend). Structure mirrors the five-step method so the plan is a lightweight version of the paid deliverable. Target ICP: founders & startups primarily; strategy-curious SMB owners and nonprofits secondarily. The one insight: a real, dated, honest-sized plan on one page — proof that a plan doesn't need to be a 40-page deck. Handoff: the printed plan's footer and the result screen both say the quiet part: 'This is the starter version. The full version — with your positioning, offer math, and the plan pressure-tested — is the $3,000 strategy engagement. A free call tells you whether it's worth it for you.' It is the most direct on-ramp to the one-time strategy offer.
- CROSS-CUTTING BUILD RULES: (a) every tool states 'Free. No email. No login. Nothing to download.' on its start screen — it is true and it is a weapon; (b) 8-12 questions max, one per screen, progress bar, under 3 minutes; (c) every result screen has exactly one CTA (book a call) tied to the specific result, never a bare 'Contact us'; (d) pass the result summary into the booking link as a query param so calls start pre-briefed; (e) all comparative claims stay qualitative ('the step most owner-run businesses score lowest on') unless backed by the cited budget sources — no invented benchmarks; (f) the tools are portfolio evidence for the Technical practice — polish them like client work and say so ('We built this. We build things like it for clients.'); (g) per CLAUDE.md creative-batch rule, ship 2-3 of these plus copy for approval before building all five.
