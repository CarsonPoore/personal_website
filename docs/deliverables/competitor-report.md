# Competitor report: Carson Poore Consulting

Prepared Oct 9, 2026, for Carson. It draws on nine research files in `docs/research/competitors-v2/`, checked against `docs/strategy/messaging-map.md` and the live pages in this repo. Every competitor price below comes from that firm's own page unless marked otherwise. "(unverified)" means the number came from a directory, a search snippet, or an AI answer, not the firm's own site. Nobody had keyword-volume data, so every difficulty rating is a judgment call.

**The short version.** Almost nobody in Indianapolis publishes a price, and CPC publishes all of them. That is the biggest edge you have. Right now, though, nobody can see it. Google has indexed 2 old pages, Perplexity still quotes the old home page, and CPC showed up in 0 of 8 AI answers. Four project prices are low enough to make CPC look cheap, and the nonprofit rate is still a phrase with no number. The fastest gains come from getting indexed and listed, fixing those prices, and rebuilding one page around "fractional CMO Indianapolis."

## 4. Positioning

**What every competitor already claims (don't lead with these):** "full-service," "data-driven," "results," "custom, not cookie-cutter," "no long-term contracts" (nobody explains the exit), "dedicated team," "24/7 while you sleep," unsourced savings percentages, self-assigned "#1" badges, and "Let's talk" as the only next step.

**Gaps only CPC can own today:**
- A complete published price list across marketing, technical, strategy and projects. No Indy firm has one.
- Written terms: 3 months, then month to month, and an 8-client cap. No local competitor publishes either. TBH requires a year.
- Ownership and exit. Only Web Tonic says the Ads account is the client's. Nobody covers the domain, GA4, GTM, GBP, or what gets handed over at exit.
- Strategy and build on one team: marketing plus web, GA4, GHL and AI at small-business prices. Nobody in any group bundles all of it.
- Local Ad Grants management at a stated nonprofit rate. No Indiana specialist found, and no rival advertises a nonprofit rate.
- A written AI oversight policy. Only Lotiva comes close ([pricing](https://lotiva.com/pricing/ai-automation/)).

**Five sharper claims, with evidence:**
1. **"Every price is on the site, including the ones other firms make you call for."** Evidence: 8 of 10 Indy agencies, all 3 local fractional CMOs and most Ad Grants specialists publish nothing.
2. **"A fractional CMO who also does the work, from $2,400 a month. Published rates selling into Indianapolis start at $8,000."** Evidence: [MarkCMO](https://markcmo.com/fractional-cmo/indiana/indianapolis/). MarketerHire puts the category at $3,000–$15,000. Say "many fractional CMOs are paid to direct, not to do." Don't say "most": Dala, Madelyn Victoria, TBH, Marketing Guardians and Media Matters all claim they execute.
3. **"Three months, then month to month. Leave whenever you like, and everything stays in your name."** Evidence: TBH's one-year minimum, and REACHRIGHT's 12-month commitment to waive its setup fee. Nobody else publishes ownership or exit terms. This claim needs the ownership page and the contract clause behind it.
4. **"Google Ad Grant management from Indianapolis, at a published nonprofit rate."** Evidence: no Indiana specialist surfaced, and none of the national firms read on their own sites offers a nonprofit rate. Don't say "only" or "first" until Google's Certified Professional Agency directory has been checked.
5. **"AI that asks before it sends, with a written policy."** Evidence: the field sells autonomy ("while you sleep"), and only Lotiva documents human approval.

**Weak spots, plainly:**
- **No case studies.** TBH shows named numbers (OrthoIndy +80% appointment requests, self-reported). CPC can't, yet. Bridge with process transparency, the free tools, and cited industry data. Never invent proof.
- **No reviews.** Clutch ranks on reviews, and AI answers lean on Clutch.
- **New domain, barely indexed.** 2 old pages in Google.
- **Small team.** "Dedicated team capacity" (the $4,800 tier) and "full team behind it" will be tested on the first sales call. Make sure they're true as written.
- **No Google Business Profile.** Local AI answers pull from directories keyed to NAP and GBP.
- **Split identity.** Carson's LinkedIn lists Vertical Studios and Copperleaf, not CPC. There are also name collisions with Carson LLP and carson.marketing.

## 9. Risks and open questions

**Decisions for Carson:**
1. **The nonprofit rate number.** Pick a percentage off list (the research suggests a percentage rather than a lower base price). Also decide whether to offer Ad Grants-only management near the $600 market top. That client would take one of 8 slots for $600 a month.
2. **The $1,200 tier's label.** Selling it as "fractional CMO" invites "too cheap to be senior" when published rates start at $2,500–$8,000. Options: call $1,200 "one channel, run properly" and present the fractional CMO from $2,400, or keep the current wording.
3. **Approve the project price changes** in section 3, especially the $350 shoot and the $6,000 web app.
4. **The 8-client cap math.** Eight clients at $1,200 is $9,600 a month. Eight at $4,800 is $38,400. Project and strategy clients also count toward the cap, so a few $900 sites can fill it. Should small projects count?
5. **Suburb and industry pages.** Two research files recommend real suburb pages. The guardrail bans per-suburb doorway pages. I recommend keeping the guardrail and allowing industry spokes only.
6. **Naming competitors on the site.** I recommend comparing against cited market ranges, not named Indy firms.

**Risks:**
- **Seller-sourced benchmarks.** Most market ranges come from vendors' own pages, not neutral surveys. Directory figures (Plaid, Radius, video minimums) and AI-snippet prices (Newton's $6K–$15K) are unverified.
- **Unconfirmed GHL details.** Re-check the $497 tier and Starter's sub-account limit in a browser before publishing.
- **Ad Grants copy accuracy.** The live FAQ cites a one-month login rule that current Google pages don't support. Policy-wrong copy hurts trust with exactly the buyers who check.
- **"Only" claims.** Happy To Help (Fishers) markets Ad Grants, and Google's certified directory hasn't been checked.
- **Gaps in the evidence.** Only Perplexity was tested, each prompt ran once, and no ranking or volume data exists.
