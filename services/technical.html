Eleven tabs, none of them talking

Here’s the owner we meet most weeks. The booking tool doesn’t talk to the invoicing tool. The customer list lives in three places and none of them agree. A lead comes in at 4pm Friday and gets a reply Tuesday, if the sticky note survives the weekend. Nothing is broken, exactly. It’s all just separate.

Founders arrive from the other direction. You’d rather build the product than the ops, and you want one person doing the work of three. The honest route there is plumbing: follow-up that runs itself and numbers that are right the first time.

That’s this practice. If you’ve been searching for a marketing automation consultant, you’re in the right place: we connect the tools you already own, build whatever’s missing, and keep a person in charge of anything a customer will ever see.

And it travels. Marketing and local SEO are Indianapolis work; the technical practice runs nationwide, over a screen share. A CRM doesn’t care what state you’re in.

One opinion before the list below. We think most small businesses buy new software about a year before they need it. The real problem is usually the handoff between the tools they already pay for, so we check that first. It’s cheaper, and it’s often enough.

A human approves every send

We’ll say the quiet part first: plenty of small-business AI projects fail, and the ones we’ve seen share a pattern. Someone automated a process nobody had mapped, then let it talk to customers unsupervised.

So we run it differently. One task first. Mapped before it’s built. And nothing reaches a customer without a person approving the send, not a text, not a review request. Slower on day one. Much better by month three.

The other difference is where the builds come from. The plan comes first, whether that’s the $3,000 strategy engagement or the five steps inside a retainer, and this practice builds what the plan calls for. If the shiny tool isn’t on the plan, we don’t buy it. Neither should you.

What people ask before they buy builds

What does a marketing automation consultant do?

Connects the tools a business already uses (CRM, calendar, phones, email, website) so leads get followed up automatically and nobody retypes a name they already have. We also build the missing pieces: AI agents for inbox triage and scheduling, missed-call text-back, review requests. Every automated message is approved by a person before it sends.

Do you work with businesses outside Indianapolis?

Yes. Marketing and local SEO are Indianapolis-area work, but the technical practice runs nationwide. CRM setups, websites, automations, and analytics all happen over a screen share, and most technical clients never need us in the room.

Will AI send messages to my customers without me seeing them?

No. That’s the rule the whole practice is built on: a person approves anything a customer will see before it goes out. Automation does the drafting and the remembering. People do the sending.

What does technical work cost?

Ongoing work runs on the same published retainers as everything we do: $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800 a month, with a three-month minimum and the whole consultancy capped at eight clients at a time. One-off builds get a fixed written quote before work starts. Nonprofits get the reduced rate listed on the pricing page.

Do we have to use GoHighLevel?

No. When your current stack works, we build on it. We recommend GoHighLevel when a business is paying for five or more tools that don’t talk to each other, because consolidating usually costs less and breaks less. If what you have is fine, we’ll say so.
