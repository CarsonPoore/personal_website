Be the answer when customers search or ask.

SEO for a small business is simpler than the industry makes it sound. Your site has to load fast and be readable by Google. Then it has to answer the questions your customers actually type. We do that work for small businesses in Indianapolis, plus the newer piece: making it more likely your business gets named when someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity who to call.

Here’s what SEO actually is.

Search engine optimization means making your website easy for Google to find and trust, so it shows up when someone types a question you can answer. That’s it. Most of the job is housekeeping and honest writing. What’s changed is where people ask. Some of your customers now type the question into ChatGPT instead of Google, and those tools read the web much the way Google does. They look for a page that answers the question directly.

This is your lane if
  • Your site launched a few years ago and nobody has looked under the hood since.
  • Customers ask the same questions before they buy, and your site answers none of them.
  • You’ve searched for your own service and found weaker competitors ranked above you.
  • You can wait a few months for results that keep paying after.
Probably not, if
  • You need calls this month. SEO won’t get you there. Ads will.
  • Your Google Business Profile is still half-empty. Fix that first with local SEO. It’s the quicker win.
  • You want a guaranteed ranking. We don’t sell one.

We think most small-business websites don’t need more pages. They need the pages they have to answer the question.

The work, in the order we do it.

Foundations first, then writing, then the steady upkeep. You see every piece of it in a monthly report.

Make sure Google can get in

Before anyone writes a word, we check the plumbing. How fast the site loads on a phone. Broken links. Pages accidentally hidden from search. A clean sitemap. Redirects left over from your last redesign. Then we add structured data, a bit of code that hands search engines your business name, services, hours, and service area in a format they read without guessing. It isn’t glamorous. It’s often the reason a decent site never shows up.

Pages that answer what customers ask

We start with the questions you hear on the phone. What does it cost? How long does it take? Do you come out to Fishers? Each one gets a page or a section that answers in the first two sentences and then shows the detail. People read that way, and it’s the shape Google and the AI tools tend to quote. No keyword stuffing. No 2,000-word filler written for robots.

Links between your pages that make sense

Your pages should point to each other the way a good employee points a customer: from the service to the price, from one question to the next one they’ll have. Visitors find what they need, and Google learns which pages matter most.

Search Console, Bing, and a monthly report

We set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools in your name and submit your sitemap to both. Bing is worth the ten minutes, because its index feeds Microsoft Copilot and is one of the sources behind ChatGPT’s search. Each month you get a short report in plain English: the searches that brought people in, the pages that led to calls or form fills, and what we’re changing next.

Getting named in AI answers, honestly.

Nobody can guarantee that ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI Overviews will cite your business. Anyone who promises it is guessing. What we can tell you is what these tools tend to pull from. When we ran a set of Indianapolis questions through Perplexity this fall, the “who should I hire” answers leaned almost entirely on directories and review sites like Clutch, BBB, and Thumbtack. The “how much does it cost” answers quoted small firms’ own pages, the ones written to answer one question with a number up top.

So the work is plain. Answer-first pages on your own site. The same business name, address, and phone everywhere you’re listed. Schema markup, so machines read your details correctly. An llms.txt file, a short plain-text guide to your site for AI crawlers. (It’s cheap to add, and honestly, nobody knows yet how much it helps.) And listings on the directories those answers draw from. Then every month we run a fixed set of real prompts your customers might type and report what changed: whether you showed up, who did instead, and which page got quoted.

SEO is the slowest channel we run.

We’d rather say that now than in month two. Technical fixes can show up in Search Console within a few weeks. New pages usually take a few months to settle into rankings, and longer in a crowded category. AI answers move on their own schedule, since each tool re-reads the web when it decides to. If you need booked work this quarter, Google Ads is faster, and we’ll tell you so.

The payoff for waiting is real, though. A page that earns its spot keeps bringing people in for years, with no charge per click. And if you suspect Google can’t see you at all, start with why businesses vanish from Google.

Including us. If you hear any of these on a sales call, keep your wallet closed.

A guaranteed ranking

Questions we get about SEO and AI search.

How long until SEO works?

Expect months, not weeks. Technical fixes can show up in Search Console within a few weeks, and new pages usually need a few months to settle into rankings. Crowded categories take longer. We report every month, so you’ll see the early signs, like more pages indexed and more searches showing your site, before the calls pick up.

Do you guarantee rankings?

No. Google itself says nobody can guarantee a top ranking, and we agree. What we stand behind is the work: what we changed and what moved, in a plain report every month. If someone promises you page one, ask them which search, and how many people actually type it.

What is AI search optimization, and does it work?

It’s making your business easy for tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google’s AI Overviews to find and quote. In practice that means answer-first pages, the same business details everywhere, schema markup, and listings on the directories those tools cite. Does it work? It makes a citation more likely. Nobody can promise one. We test a fixed set of real prompts each month and show you what changed, including when nothing did.

How is this different from local SEO?

Local SEO is your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and the map results. This is your website: speed, structure, and pages that answer real questions. They feed each other, and most local businesses need both. If you’re not sure where you stand, run Can Indy Find You?, our free visibility check, first.

Do I need a blog?

Probably not the way you’re picturing it. You don’t need a post every week. You need pages that answer what customers ask before they buy, and those can live as service pages, an FAQ, or a few solid articles. If writing them turns into a regular job, that’s content marketing, and we do that too.
