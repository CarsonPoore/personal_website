Your own shop beats stock every time.

A stock photo of a grinning stranger in a hard hat tells a customer nothing. They may have already seen him on a competitor’s site. A photo of your own truck parked outside the deck you finished last week tells them exactly who’s showing up. That’s the part that earns the call.

It doesn’t need to be glossy. Sawdust on the bench is fine. So is a lunch rush that looks like a lunch rush. People can tell when a business is real, and they trust it more for it.

We think real photos of your shop, your crew, and your customers beat stock every time. Even the slightly messy ones.

How a shoot runs, start to files.

No surprises on the day. Most of the work happens before anyone picks up a camera.

First, a plan you sign off on

We start with a call about what you sell and who buys it. Then we write a shot list and, for video, a short script in your words. You approve both before we book the day. If something on the list feels off, it comes off.

Shoot day happens at your place

We come to you, anywhere in central Indiana. Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Avon, downtown. We work around your hours and your customers, and we keep the gear footprint small so the shop still runs.

You see it before it’s final

For video, you get a first cut and a chance to send notes. For photos, we cull the set, edit the keepers, and show you the lot. Nothing is “done” until you say so.

Files land in a folder you control

Sized for where they’ll actually go: wide for your website, vertical for phones. And they’re yours. No licensing fee next year, no asking us for your own footage.

One great video won’t carry your marketing.

Here’s the honest part. A beautiful video with nowhere to live ends up on a YouTube channel nobody visits. It needs a home: a website that loads fast on a phone, and social accounts that post more than twice a year. Without those, you’ve paid for something lovely that almost no one sees.

So if your site or your feed isn’t ready, we’ll say so before you buy a shoot. We build those too. Start with web design and development or social media, and the video has somewhere to go.

Questions owners ask before the camera shows up.

What happens on shoot day?

Less than you’d think. You approved the shot list days earlier, so nobody is improvising. We show up, set up quietly, and work through the list: the space, the work in progress, your people doing what they do, and a few portraits if you want them. A half-day photo shoot is about four hours. Video can run longer, and your quote says how long. You can usually stay open while we work.

How long until I get the files?

Your quote puts a delivery date in writing before anything starts. Photos come back first, usually within two weeks of the shoot. Video takes longer because you see a first cut, send us your notes, and approve the final before we call it done.

Do I own the footage and photos?

Yes. The footage, the finished videos, and the photos are yours. Put them on your site, your Google profile, your ads, a flyer, a screen at a trade show. They land in a folder you control, so you never have to ask us for your own files.

Can you shoot at my business?

That’s the whole idea. We shoot on location in central Indiana: your shop floor, kitchen, job site, office, or studio, from Westfield down to Greenwood. A studio backdrop makes you look like everybody else. Your own place doesn’t.

Do I need to be on camera?

No. A good intro video can run on hands at work, your crew, your customers, and a voiceover. That said, people like buying from a person, and twenty honest seconds from the owner tends to beat anything polished. We plan around what you’re comfortable with, and we write the words with you so you’re never winging it.
