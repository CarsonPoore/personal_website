Your Google Business Profile, actually finished

Most profiles we open are half-done: no description, three photos from 2019, a category that’s close but wrong. We fill every field. The most specific category Google offers, all 750 characters of description, your services listed, photos that look like your real work, a post or two a month. Your website link matches your site exactly, down to the trailing slash.

A review system that runs itself

A steady flow of recent reviews now counts for more than a big old pile of them. So we build the ask into your workflow: when a job wraps or a table clears, the customer gets a text with one link. We draft replies to every review, good and rough, in your voice. No begging, no fake accounts, ever.

One name, address, and number, everywhere

Google cross-checks your business details against Yelp, Facebook, Bing, and Apple, and every mismatch quietly costs you trust. We hunt down each listing and make them identical. Honestly? This part is data entry. It’s boring. We do it anyway, because Google notices.

Local signals on your website

Your site should answer what people around here actually type: the service and the town, in plain words near the top of the page, with the cost question answered instead of dodged. We write those pages, put the city where it belongs, and add schema markup — code that tells Google what your business is, in Google’s own language. It works in Carmel and Fishers the same way it works in Greenwood, Brownsburg, and Avon.

What owners ask us before they start.

How long does local SEO take to work?

Longer than anyone selling it wants to admit. If your profile has been neglected, basic fixes can move your map ranking within a few weeks. In a crowded category in a dense part of Indy, it takes months of steady signals. That’s one reason for our three-month minimum: we won’t charge you for a month in which nothing could honestly happen. Every month you get a plain-English report showing what moved.

Do I need to pay for a Google Business Profile?

No. The profile is free, and claiming it is free. If someone calls offering to “activate” or “secure” your listing for a fee, hang up. What costs money is the ongoing work of keeping it complete, photographed, posted, and reviewed, which is the work we do.

Should I do local SEO or Google Ads first?

Local SEO first, almost every time. It is the cheaper, more durable win, and ads perform better once your profile and website are ready to catch what the ads send. When you are ready for that step, Google Ads is its own service with the same published pricing.

What if I have almost no reviews?

Then this matters more for you, not less. Profiles with 40 or more reviews averaging 4.5 stars appear in the map pack about 3.7 times more often than profiles with fewer than 10, per BrightLocal. The fix is a system, not begging: ask at the moment the customer is happiest, by text, with one link, every time. We set it up so it runs without you thinking about it.

Can I just do this myself?

A lot of it, yes. We built a free 18-point self-check so you can: run it, fix what you can in an afternoon, and don’t pay anyone for that part. Owners usually hire us when the slow, repetitive pieces (listings cleanup, review replies, photos, monthly upkeep) stop happening because business picked up. Which is sort of the point.
