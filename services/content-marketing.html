You already answer these questions all day.

On the phone, at the counter, in a quote you emailed last Tuesday. Content marketing is writing those answers down once, well, where strangers can find them. It fits owners who know their trade cold and never have time to write about it. It fits nonprofits too, whose donors keep asking where the money goes.

This is your lane if
  • Customers ask the same five questions before they buy, and you’re tired of typing the answers.
  • Your website says what you do but never explains why anyone should pick you.
  • You have an email list and haven’t sent it anything in a year.
  • You can spare half an hour a month to talk. We handle the writing.
Probably not, if
  • You need calls this month. Content won’t do that. Local SEO or ads will get there sooner.
  • You want a post every day for the algorithm. We’ll write less, and better.
  • Nobody can find you on Google at all yet. Fix that first, then publish.

We think two useful articles a month beat twelve thin ones. Every single time.

Here’s what lands on your site each month.

Every piece starts from a question a real customer asked you. Then it gets used everywhere it can be.

Articles that answer one question properly

What does a new roof cost in Carmel? Should a nonprofit hire or outsource its books? We pick the questions your customers ask right before they buy, and write one clear, specific answer per article, with your real prices and your real opinions in it. Sometimes a topic deserves more room, so it becomes a longer guide people bookmark and forward.

Written so Google and ChatGPT can quote you

More people now ask AI answer engines like ChatGPT before they ever open a search page. Those tools pull from pages that answer a question plainly near the top, name a place, and show who wrote it. So that’s how we structure every piece: the answer first, the detail after, your name and city on it, and schema markup so search engines know what they’re reading.

A newsletter people open

Once a month, a short email to the people who already know you. One useful thing, plus one clear reason to reply or book. It’s the cheapest repeat business there is, and most owners we meet have a list sitting unused in a spreadsheet.

One piece, cut up for social

An article that took a week to get right shouldn’t be posted once and forgotten. We cut each one into a handful of short posts and a line for your Google profile. If you want social media run as its own channel, that’s a separate conversation.

This is slow. We’d rather say so now.

Content compounds over months, not weeks. The first article you publish probably won’t ring the phone. The fifteenth might, because by then there’s a library that answers most of what your customers wonder, and Google has had time to trust it. If you need booked work by the end of the quarter, we’ll point you somewhere faster and tell you why.

The upside is that it keeps working. An article that answers “how much does this cost in Indianapolis” can still be bringing in calls two years after we wrote it.

Questions owners ask about content

How often should a small business publish?

Less often than you’ve been told. Our default is two articles a month and one newsletter, and we’d rather drop to one great article than pad the schedule. Consistency matters more than volume. A business that publishes twice a month for a year is in a much better spot than one that posts daily for six weeks and then goes quiet.

Who actually writes it?

We do. You give us about half an hour a month on a call, we ask what customers asked you lately, and we turn your answers into drafts. You read every piece before it goes live and you can change anything. Your expertise, our writing. Nobody needs to know which sentences were whose.

Does AI-written content work?

Unedited, no. It reads like everyone else’s, because it was built from everyone else’s. Google has said it judges content on whether it’s helpful, not on how it was produced, and generic answers aren’t helpful. We use AI for research and rough outlines. A person who knows your business writes and edits every piece, and the parts that make it worth reading (your prices and your opinions) can only come from you.

How long until content pays off?

Months. Most articles take a while to get picked up by search, and the library has to get deep enough to matter. A newsletter can work faster if you already have a customer list, since those people know you. That’s part of why our retainers have a three-month minimum. Each monthly report shows what was read, what got found, and what led to a call.

Do you do email newsletters?

Yes, and for many local businesses it’s the better first channel. We write it, design a simple template, and send it from the tool you already use, or set one up if you don’t have one. Monthly is the usual rhythm. If the list lives in a CRM, we can connect it to the rest of your follow-up too.
