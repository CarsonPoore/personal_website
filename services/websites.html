Most small businesses get sold more website than they need. A fast five-page site with a booking link beats a forty-page build nobody updates.

Why we lead with what happens if you leave.

This industry has a pattern that never appears in proposals: the hostage website. The agency registers your domain and hosts your site in their account. Your Google Business Profile? “Managed” from their login. Everything works fine. Right up until you cancel.

It gets expensive fast. When an owner ends the contract, the agency can cut off the hosting and the phone number tied to the Google profile, and the phones go dark. So we build the other way around: your accounts, your card on the billing, our hands on the keyboard. If we stopped working together tomorrow, nothing would turn off.

What stays yours, in writing
  • The domain, registered in your own account
  • The hosting, billed to your card, not ours
  • Every file, in a repository you can hand to any developer
  • Your Google Business Profile, with you as primary owner
  • Your analytics and ad accounts, same arrangement

Built to load before doubt sets in.

Your customers are on phones, often on parking-lot signal. A page that hesitates loses them to the next result. So we build static pages wherever we can, with no plugin stack and no page builder dragging forty scripts behind it. Just files that show up instantly. You’re reading one right now. This site is built exactly the way we build client sites.

There’s a newer reason too. AI answer engines increasingly decide which local businesses get mentioned, and our bet is that clean, fast pages that state plainly what you do and what it costs are the ones that get read and quoted. A quick site used to be polish. We treat it as positioning.

One honest limit: a fast site can’t rescue a business nobody searches for. If the deeper problem is being found at all, start with local SEO instead. It’s cheaper, and it’s the first thing we check for every local business we work with.

How a build actually goes.

Four stages, no mystery phase. You’ll know where your project sits every week, because you can see the site taking shape at a real link.

1

Words before design

We write the site first. What you do, where you do it, what it costs, why you. A confession: this is the slow part, and it’s slower than we’d like. Turning a business into plain words takes longer than choosing colors. It also matters more.

2

Build in your accounts

Domain and hosting go in your name before the first page exists, and the code lives in a repository you control. You watch the site come together at a live preview link, and feedback happens there, on real screens, not in mockups.

3

Launch and point everything at it

Old links redirect. Your Google Business Profile matches the new site exactly. And analytics gets wired in from the first visitor, so when the phone rings, you can see which page made it happen.

4

Hand over, or stick around

You get a walkthrough of how to change your own text and hours. Some owners take it from there. Done. Others fold ongoing care into a monthly retainer with the rest of their marketing. Both are fine with us.

The questions worth asking any web shop.

How much does a small business website cost in Indianapolis?

Starter sites start at $900, business sites at $2,500, and web apps at $6,000. The final number depends on the page count and the features, and on how much of the writing falls to us. You get it in writing before any work starts, and it holds unless you change the scope. Every project price is on the investment page.

Do I own my website if we stop working together?

Yes. All of it. The domain is registered in your account and the hosting is billed to your card. Every file lives in a repository you control. We build it that way from day one, so there is nothing to transfer when you leave and nothing we could hold. If we parted ways tomorrow, your site wouldn’t notice.

How long does a website take to build?

A marketing site usually takes three to four weeks. The writing is the long pole, not the design, and it goes faster when you can give us an hour of honest answers about what you do and what it costs. A booking flow adds a week or two. Web apps are their own animal: we scope them individually and give you a timeline with the quote, and if it’s a months-long build we say so up front.

Can you fix my current website instead of rebuilding it?

Often, yes, and we’d rather. Some sites need an afternoon of fixes (slow images, a homepage that never says what the business does) and not a rebuild. On a free call we’ll look at yours together and tell you which kind it is. If the answer is “keep what you have,” you’ll hear that too.

What do you build websites with?

Plain, fast pages wherever possible, and a light framework when a project genuinely needs one. WordPress enters only if you arrive with it and want to stay. The reason is boring and practical: fewer moving parts means faster load times and fewer plugin emergencies at 2 a.m. Whatever we build with, the files and accounts are yours.
