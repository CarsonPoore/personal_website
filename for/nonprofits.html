Nonprofit marketing for the person already wearing five hats.

For executive and development directors across central Indiana. We start with the free money Google already sets aside for you, then build the donor list, the stories, and the board numbers that let a small staff work like a bigger one.

Marketing falls to whoever has ten spare minutes.

You’re the executive director. You’re also the development director, the volunteer coordinator, the grant writer, and on Thursdays, the entire marketing department. Nobody on staff has marketing as their actual job. So it happens in the gaps between a site visit and a grant report, when it happens at all.

We hear the same few sentences from almost every small nonprofit we sit down with:

  • The newsletter goes out when someone has time.
  • Our donor data is in three spreadsheets, and nobody trusts any of them.
  • We have the Google Grant. Honestly, nobody’s touched it in months.
  • Our development director left, and everything lived in her head.

None of that is a character flaw. Giving is thinning out, and the numbers show it. The Fundraising Effectiveness Project’s 2025 data shows the number of donors down 3.6%, with the smallest givers dropping the most. Fewer people are carrying more of the load. Community-sized organizations feel that first, because those small gifts are their base.

Not sure where your gaps are? The five-step check-up scores you in a few minutes. No email, no login.

Some board members want every dollar on programs. A few are openly wary of mixing marketing with mission. That’s a fair instinct, and you’ll need an answer for it. Here’s the one we’d hand them:

A donor found in October is a family served in March.

Google sets aside $10,000 a month in ads for you.

Through Google for Nonprofits, verified 501(c)(3) organizations can get up to $10,000 a month in free search ads. That’s Google’s figure, and the program is still running in 2026. Most organizations that have it use only a sliver.

The rules are why. Ads have to hold a 5% click-through rate. Someone has to log in every month. Conversion tracking has to be set up, and single-word keywords aren’t allowed. Miss one and Google suspends the account, usually without anyone on staff noticing for weeks.

We think the Ad Grant is the most overlooked free resource a small nonprofit has. It’s also the right first step because it costs you nothing to find out where you stand. Read how the Ad Grant works, or see how we manage it.

What the checkup answers

  1. Are you eligible? Faith-based and community organizations are welcome; Google has its own verification path there, so we confirm it with you instead of promising it here.
  2. Is your account active, or quietly suspended?
  3. How much of the $10,000 did you actually use last month?
  4. What would it take to use more of it, and point it at donors or volunteers?

Then the work that keeps a small staff from drowning

Our opinion: most small nonprofits should fix their donor list before they touch their logo. So that’s the order we work in.

One donor list everyone trusts

Your donors live in spreadsheets, an email tool, the payment processor, and whatever the event platform exported last spring. We pull them into one CRM, clean out the duplicates, and tag gifts by fund and campaign. Then the thank-you notes and lapsed-donor emails go out on schedule even when nobody remembers. This part is tedious. We do it first anyway, because every email and every report reads from this list.

What EDs and development directors ask us

How does a nonprofit get the Google Ad Grant?

First you verify your 501(c)(3) through Google for Nonprofits, then you apply for Ad Grants inside that account. Once approved, you get up to $10,000 a month in search ads, as long as the account follows Google’s rules. Faith-based organizations have their own verification path, so we confirm eligibility during the free checkup instead of promising it up front.

Why did Google suspend our Ad Grant account?

Usually one of three things: the account fell below the 5% click-through rate, nobody logged in for a month, or conversion tracking was never set up. All three are fixable. We look at which one it was during the checkup and tell you what reactivation takes.

How much is the nonprofit rate?

It’s a reduced version of our published retainers, which are $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800 a month. We hold it for a few organizations each quarter. The exact number depends on which tier fits, and you’ll hear it on the first call, before anyone writes a proposal.

Can a capacity-building grant pay for marketing help?

Sometimes. United Way of Central Indiana awards capacity-building grants, and Indiana’s community foundations cover every county. We can’t promise a grant comes through, but we’ll help you write the case for the engagement if you decide to apply.

How do I explain marketing spend to my board?

Tie it to the people you serve. A donor found in October is a family served in March. We also build a one-page monthly report showing new donors and where they came from, Ad Grant traffic included, so the board sees the work instead of taking it on faith.
