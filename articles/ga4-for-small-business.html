GA4 setup for small business, minus the clutter

What to turn on, which two reports to read, and the setup mistakes that leave small-business numbers wrong.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

Set up GA4 in an afternoon: install the tag, extend data retention to 14 months, exclude your own visits, and mark the two to five actions that precede a paycheck as key events. Then read two reports each month, Traffic acquisition and Landing page. Skip the rest.

We sell analytics work, and the honest truth is that most small businesses need about an hour of it, done correctly once. The hour is below.

What should you set up first?

Six steps, in this order.

  1. Create a GA4 property and a web data stream for your site.
  2. Install the Google tag on every page. Most site builders have a field for the measurement ID. If you use Google Tag Manager instead, install through one or the other, never both.
  3. Leave enhanced measurement on. Google’s help page lists what it collects: page views, scrolls, outbound clicks, site search, video engagement, file downloads, and form interactions.
  4. Extend data retention. Google says standard properties can keep user-level and event-level data for up to 14 months. Analytics Mania notes that new properties show only the last 2 months in explorations by default, and that a longer setting isn’t retroactive. Change it today.
  5. Define your office’s IP address as internal traffic, then set the data filter to Active.
  6. Mark your key events.

Steps 4 and 5 are the ones people skip. Both punish you later, because neither can be undone after the fact.

What is a key event, and which ones do you need?

A key event is a recorded action that matters to your business: a form submitted, a booking made, a donation completed. If an older guide tells you to “mark conversions,” that’s the old name. Google’s help page says those events are now called key events. “Conversion” now refers to an action you use to measure ad campaign performance, mostly in Google Ads. Google made the change in 2024.

Anything GA4 collects can become a key event. In the Admin area, open Events and star the ones that count. Menus move around, so if you don’t see it, search Admin for “key events.”

What you run Key event worth marking How it gets counted
A service business Contact form submitted Often caught by enhanced measurement. Test it yourself
A service business Tap on your phone number A custom event on the link, or call tracking
Anyone with a booking tool Booking confirmed A thank-you page view works almost anywhere
A nonprofit Donation completed, volunteer signed up The thank-you page after each

Keep the list short. Two to five. Mark every click and the number you care about drowns in the ones you don’t.

Which reports are worth opening?

Two, once a month. A third check is a one-time test.

Where did your visitors come from, and did they act?

That’s Traffic acquisition, under Reports, then Acquisition. It credits each visit to its own source, including repeat visits. Its neighbor, User acquisition, credits only how a person first found you, per Measure School’s walkthrough. For a small business, Traffic acquisition answers the better question: what’s driving visits that turn into work?

Look at the key events column, which replaced the old conversions column, not at sessions. Ten visits from a local directory that lead to two calls beat a thousand from social that lead to none.

Which pages start visits, and which of them lead anywhere?

That’s the Landing page report, under Engagement. Analytify’s guide describes it as a table of sessions, active users, new users, average engagement time, key events, and revenue for each page where visits begin. Find your busiest landing page. If it earns almost no key events, it’s missing a clear next step. Fix that page before you write another blog post.

Are your key events actually counting?

Test them yourself. Open Realtime, submit your own contact form, tap your own phone number, and watch the events appear. Do this once after setup and again after any website change. It takes five minutes and catches the most common failure.

Which setup mistakes cost small businesses the most?

Nobody marked a key event. GA4 records the form submission, then waits for a person to flag it. Until then the report reads zero in a week when the phone never stopped. It’s the cause we find most. The fix takes a minute.

Your own visits are counted. Google’s help page says an internal traffic filter has to be Active, not Testing, before it excludes anything, that it can take 24 to 36 hours to apply, and that excluded data is never processed. Run it in Testing first, check the results, then switch it on.

Retention stayed at the default. Standard reports aren’t affected by the retention setting, but explorations are, and Google says key events data follows the user-level setting of 2 or 14 months. Data you didn’t keep can’t be recovered.

The tag fires twice, or an old one lingers. When a site-builder plugin and Tag Manager both install GA4, page views double. And Universal Analytics, which GA4 replaced, stopped processing hits on standard properties on July 1, 2023. A leftover snippet does nothing for you.

You expect GA4 to see what it can’t. Ad blockers hide some visitors from every analytics tool. Calls placed from your website look like nothing happened without call tracking. And GA4 and Google Ads each credit sales by their own rules, so their totals won’t match. Nobody’s lying. Pick one set of totals and report by it every month.

How often should you look at GA4?

Once a month, for twenty minutes. Open Traffic acquisition and Landing page, set the date range to the last full month, and ask three plain questions. What brought in work? What did it cost, if you’re spending anywhere? What will you change next?

Write the answers in a note, one line each. Next month, compare. That note is the whole dashboard. Checking GA4 daily mostly teaches you to worry about numbers that move for no reason, and a slow Tuesday isn’t a trend.

When can you skip most of this?

If your business runs on referrals and phone calls, with a handful of web leads a month, thirty minutes is enough: steps 1 through 3 and one key event. Spend the rest of the afternoon asking for reviews. Our guide to why you’re not showing up on Google covers where that effort pays.

Don’t pay anyone to build a dashboard. Forty tiles look like work. One plain monthly page that answers what brought in work, what it cost, and what to change next is worth more.

Spending on ads changes the answer. Tracking comes before the budget, because otherwise you can’t tell which dollars worked. What else do owners ask about GA4?

Is GA4 free for small businesses?

Yes. Google’s own comparison page lists the standard version of Google Analytics as free, and it’s what nearly every small business uses. Google also sells a paid enterprise tier called Analytics 360, which most small businesses will never need.

Why does GA4 show zero conversions?

Usually because no one marked an event as a key event. GA4 renamed conversions to key events in 2024, so older guides use the old word. Open Admin, find Events, and star the actions that matter. Then submit your own form and check Realtime.

Do I need Google Tag Manager for GA4?

No. Many site builders let you paste a measurement ID and be done. Tag Manager helps when you want custom events, like taps on a phone number. Whichever you choose, install the tag through one method only, or page views double.

How long does GA4 keep my data?

Standard properties can keep user-level and event-level data for up to 14 months, and new properties start shorter. Standard reports aren’t affected, but explorations are. Change the setting right away, because a longer period isn’t retroactive.
