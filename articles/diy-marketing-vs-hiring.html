Should I do my own marketing or hire someone?

A five-minute test by hours, skill, and stakes, the honest cases where doing it yourself wins, and what hiring costs next to your own time.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

Do it yourself while revenue is small and you can give marketing about 5 steady hours a week. Hire help when those hours disappear, results sit flat for two quarters, or the next step needs a skill you don’t have. For most owners the smart split is to hire for the thinking and keep the easy parts.

Good news first. Neither answer is wrong, and the wrong one is cheap to undo. Plenty of owners do their own marketing well. Plenty hire too early and pay for a plan they can’t use. The test below sorts you out in about five minutes.

What are other owners actually doing?

Mostly squeezing it in. Fiverr surveyed nearly 6,000 small business owners in 25 countries and found 70% spend less than five hours a week on marketing. Meanwhile, vcita’s April 2025 survey of 500 US small businesses found 52% still outsource their marketing. And outsourcing doesn’t always stick. In the same survey, 40% of owners who outsource eventually dropped or switched their agency, and 60% of those cited a lack of return.

Read those together. Most owners put in few hours, and many who buy help buy it without a clear job for it. Both mistakes are fixable, and the test below catches them.

AI has changed the DIY math a little. A first draft takes minutes now. But the Census Bureau’s business survey found that fewer than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees reported using AI between December 2025 and May 2026. If you’re comfortable with the tools, that’s a real head start. A fast draft still doesn’t tell you what to say, or to whom.

A five-signal test for your situation

Answer these for this quarter, not for the version of you with a free weekend.

SignalLean toward doing it yourselfLean toward hiring
HoursYou can protect 5 hours a week, every week.Marketing gets whatever is left after everything else, which is usually nothing.
SkillYou can say who your best customer is, and why they pick you, in two sentences.The next step is ads, a site build, search setup, or automation, and you’d be learning on live money.
StakesA slow month costs you time, not cash.A bad month costs real money, like ad spend or a missed busy season.
ResultsSomething is working and you want more of it.Six months in, you can’t name a customer marketing brought you.
EnergyYou don’t mind doing it.You put it off every single week.

Three or more in the left column: keep doing it, and spend on a one-time plan instead of a monthly bill. Three or more on the right: hire. Split down the middle? Skip to the middle path below.

When does doing it yourself win?

More often than people who sell marketing like to admit. Here are the cases where we’d tell you to keep it in-house.

You’re small and busy. A solo electrician in Greenwood with a full calendar doesn’t need a retainer. A finished Google profile, a text asking each customer for a review, and one real photo a week will beat most paid help at that size.

You are the product. A coach or consultant sells trust in one person. Your voice at the networking breakfast or on LinkedIn is hard to hire out. Get help with the plumbing, like email setup and your website, and keep the voice.

You’re consistent. The owner who posts every Tuesday for two years beats the agency that posts for three months. Customers can tell the difference.

You haven’t proven the offer. If you’re not sure anyone will pay for what you sell, a retainer pays someone to polish an unproven idea. Founders in that spot should read our founders page for the homework we’d give instead.

When does hiring win?

When your hours are gone. If marketing happens in the 20 minutes after dinner, it will keep getting skipped, and a skipped plan is the same as no plan.

When the next step is a specialist’s job. Google Ads, search work, site builds, and automation reward experience, and mistakes on ads cost money by the day. Someone who has set up many ad accounts skips the errors you’d pay to find.

And when you’ve stalled. Six months of steady effort with nothing to show for it usually means the thinking is off, not the effort. Fresh eyes are cheaper than a seventh month.

What does hiring cost next to doing it yourself?

Your hours count, even when no invoice shows up. Say your time is worth $75 an hour. Five hours a week is roughly $1,500 a month. DIY is cheaper only when you’d do the work anyway and it stays steady.

For the hiring side, start with the pricey option. The median pay for advertising, promotions, and marketing managers was $165,780 a year in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. That’s about $13,800 a month in wages, before benefits and software. Our retainers are $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800 a month, three months minimum, and every tier is on our pricing page. At $300K in annual revenue, the $1,200 tier is 4.8% of sales. Our marketing budget guide runs the rest of that math.

If $1,200 a month would be your entire marketing budget, don’t hire us yet. Do it yourself, and spend that money on your Google profile and on answering every call.

What’s the middle path?

It’s where most owners we talk to end up, and it costs less than either extreme. Pay for judgment, keep the doing.

The $3,000 strategy engagement is one way. You get positioning, a defined best-fit customer, a sharp offer, and a 90-day plan you can run without us. If you move to a retainer afterward, half of it ($1,500) is credited. The other way is an advisor who reviews your work every month. Our comparison of a fractional CMO and a marketing agency covers which kind of help fills which gap.

What should you hand off first?

Hand off what’s technical, slow to learn, or expensive to get wrong: ad accounts, website builds, search setup, tracking, and automation. Keep what only you can do, which is talking to customers and showing your real work. Our rule is to give away the plumbing and keep the voice.

Whoever you hire, get ownership in writing. Your website, your Google Business Profile, and your ad accounts should be registered to you from day one.

What else do owners ask about DIY marketing?

Is it cheaper to do my own marketing?

On paper, yes. In practice it costs hours, and those hours come out of selling and serving customers. Five hours a week at $75 an hour is roughly $1,500 a month in time. DIY is cheaper when you’d do the work anyway and keep it steady. It costs more when it keeps getting dropped.

How many hours a week does marketing take?

Fiverr found 70% of small business owners spend under five hours a week on it. We think 5 steady hours is the floor for one channel done properly, like a finished Google profile, review requests, and a weekly post. Two channels done well beat five done when you remember.

Can I do some of my marketing myself and hire out the rest?

Yes, and it’s the setup we’d pick for most owners. Hand off the technical parts, like ads, site builds, and tracking. Keep customer conversations and photos of your real work. Split it by skill, not by channel.

Should I hire a freelancer, an agency, or a consultant?

Match the hire to the gap. A freelancer fits one defined job. An agency fits production at volume. A consultant or fractional CMO fits a missing plan. Our article on a fractional CMO versus a marketing agency walks through each.
