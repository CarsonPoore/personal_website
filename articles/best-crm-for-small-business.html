What’s the best CRM for a small business?

Honest picks by business type, with the prices from each company’s own pricing page, a comparison table, and a short list of owners who don’t need a CRM yet.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

The best CRM for a small business is the one that does your one most important job. For a service business that misses calls and texts, that’s GoHighLevel at $97 a month or Jobber at $49. For a sales pipeline, Pipedrive at $14 a user. To start free, HubSpot or Zoho.

Those are list prices from each company’s own pricing page, checked October 9, 2026. They change, so confirm before you buy. What doesn’t change much is the pattern: owners pick a CRM off a features page, use a sliver of it, and quietly go back to the inbox. Here’s how to avoid that.

How do you pick a CRM when every site says it does everything?

Pick by job, not by feature list. Write down the one thing that’s costing you money right now. Then buy the cheapest tool that does that thing well.

For most owners it’s one of five jobs. Answering new leads fast. Tracking quotes so none go quiet. Booking appointments without phone tag. Sending the same email to a list. Asking happy customers for reviews. Each job points to a different tool, which is why no single “best” exists.

Our opinion: if you can’t name the job in one sentence, you aren’t ready to shop. You’ll end up paying for a platform and building nothing on it.

Which CRM fits a local service business?

If you run a plumbing, HVAC, cleaning, or salon business, the lead you lose is usually the one nobody answered. You were on a roof. The caller dialed the next name on the list.

GoHighLevel is built for that. According to its pricing page, the plans run $97, $297, and $497 a month with unlimited contacts and users, and the $97 plan includes 3 sub-accounts, plenty for one business. It bundles pipelines, calendars, forms, email and text, and review requests. Texts, calls, and AI features are metered on top. We cover the full cost math in Is GoHighLevel worth it?, including who should skip it.

If your day is scheduling crews and sending invoices, look at Jobber first. Its pricing page lists Core at $49 a month for one user without a commitment, and $29 on annual billing. It does quotes, schedules, and invoices well. It isn’t a marketing platform, and it doesn’t pretend to be.

What if you’re a solo owner just getting organized?

Start free and stay free as long as you can. HubSpot’s free tools cover up to 2 users at $0. Zoho CRM’s free edition covers up to 3 users and includes workflow automation, follow-up reminders, and 5,000 API calls a day.

Most owners are moving off a spreadsheet, and that’s normal. In a 2023 survey of more than 1,500 small businesses run for Zoho by SMB Group, 56% had no CRM, and 39% of those used a mix of spreadsheets, email, and personal tools. Zoho paid for that study, so read it with that in mind. The pattern still matches what we see in Indianapolis shops.

What about a small sales team?

Pipedrive is the plain answer. It’s a pipeline tool first. Its pricing page shows Lite at $14, Growth at $39, Premium at $59, and Ultimate at $79 per seat per month on annual billing. Lite covers leads, deals, and the calendar. Growth adds email sync and automated follow-up sequences, which is where most small teams land.

Zoho CRM is the budget alternative. Its list prices run $14 for Standard, $23 for Professional, and $40 for Enterprise per user per month. Bigin, Zoho’s lighter product, starts at $7. You get a lot for the money, and you spend an afternoon deciding which settings matter.

When is HubSpot the right call?

When marketing drives your leads. Blog posts, forms, email campaigns, and a sales team that lives in the same system. Starter is $20 per seat per month on HubSpot’s sales pricing page, with a lower intro price for new customers. Sales Hub Professional is $100 per seat per month, plus a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee. Marketing Hub Professional starts at $890 a month, with a $3,000 onboarding fee, per its marketing pricing page.

That jump from $20 to $890 is the thing to understand before you start. Free and Starter are friendly. The features most owners eventually want sit a long way up the ladder. We put the two head to head, with real costs at three list sizes, in GoHighLevel vs HubSpot.

Does the CRM matter more than how fast you answer?

No. Speed beats software. A 2011 Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million leads found that firms contacting a prospect within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited one hour longer. It’s old, and the authors included an executive from a lead-response vendor. The direction has held up.

Newer numbers say owners still lose here. A 2025 test by Hennessey Digital sent inquiries to 1,333 U.S. law firms. The median reply took 13 minutes, 25% replied inside 5 minutes, and 26% never replied. Law firms aren’t your business, but the habit of letting a form sit isn’t limited to lawyers. We cover the fix in how to automate lead follow-up.

When shouldn’t you buy a CRM yet?

Under about 50 leads a month, a shared inbox, a calendar link, and a spreadsheet with a “next call” column do the job. Buying software early doesn’t fix a thin lead flow. It gives you a nicer place to look at it.

The same Zoho and SMB Group survey found that 31% of businesses without a CRM said they were too small to need one. For some of them that’s right. Wait until you find a lead in a text thread three weeks late. That’s the signal.

Also skip the switch if the CRM you have works. A tool your team opens every morning beats a better one they resent. Our Five-Step Check-Up takes about three minutes and shows whether follow-up is even your leak.

Why do small-business CRM projects fail?

People, mostly. In that same survey, 29% of non-users cited employees resisting new software, and 53% named ease of use as something they want from a CRM. A tool nobody opens is a monthly charge.

The dull parts decide it. Importing contacts without duplicates. Agreeing who owns the system after launch. We think most CRM projects die in the import, not the demo. Do that part first and slowly.

What else do owners ask about choosing a CRM?

What is the best CRM for a small business?

The one that does your most important job. A service business that misses calls and texts is usually best served by GoHighLevel at $97 a month or Jobber at $49. A small sales team fits Pipedrive from $14 per user. Starting free? HubSpot and Zoho both have free editions.

Is there a good free CRM for a small business?

Yes. HubSpot’s free tools cover up to 2 users at $0, and Zoho CRM’s free edition covers up to 3 users with workflow automation and follow-up reminders. Both get expensive when you outgrow them, so check the next tier’s price before you build your process on the free one.

Do I need a CRM at all?

Not always. Under about 50 leads a month, a shared inbox, a calendar link, and a spreadsheet can carry you. Buy one when leads start slipping, like a text you find three weeks late, or when more than one person touches your customers.

How much should a small business pay for a CRM?

You can start anywhere from $0 to about $100 a month. Flat-priced tools like GoHighLevel start at $97 a month for the whole business. Per-seat tools like Pipedrive ($14 to $79 per user) and Zoho ($14 to $40 per user) grow with headcount.

How long does it take to set up a CRM?

A working setup takes about a week: contacts imported, pipeline stages named, calendar and forms connected. A settled one takes a couple of months, because you tune it on real leads. The import and cleanup take longer than the building.

Is GoHighLevel a good CRM for small business?

For service businesses that run on calls, texts, and forms, often yes. It replaces a scheduler, email tool, forms, and review software for one flat plan, plus metered usage. For solos with few leads, usually no. Our full cost breakdown is in the article Is GoHighLevel worth it.
