What does a fractional CMO cost?

The market's published ranges, ours beside them, and a plain section on when you shouldn't hire one at all.

By Carson Poore Last updated: October 2026

Most fractional CMOs charge $3,000–$15,000 a month for 10–20 hours a week, per the published rate guides at MarketerHire, Chief Outsiders, and Kalungi. Hourly engagements run $150–$350. Our own retainers at Carson Poore Consulting are $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800 a month — published, three months minimum, then month to month.

That's the short version. The longer version is below, including the one question that matters more than price: whether you should buy this at all.

What moves the number

Three things set the price: hours, seniority, and who the provider was built to serve.

Hours first. The standard engagement is 10 to 20 hours a week, a range MarketerHire and Kalungi both publish in their own guides. Fewer hours, lower retainer. Twenty hours a week of a senior operator's time is real money anywhere you buy it.

Seniority second. Someone who has run marketing through two growth stages charges more than someone two years out of an agency job, which is why hourly rates in those same guides stretch all the way from $150 to $350.

The third one is the part nobody tells you. The market's price floor was set by startups. Nearly every firm ranking for this question serves Series A and B companies, funded, with a two-to-six-person marketing team already in place for the CMO to direct. A $5,000-a-month floor makes sense for that buyer. It makes much less sense for a $900K plumbing company in Greenwood or a two-founder software business that hasn't raised a dime.

We think most businesses under $2M in revenue get quoted prices from a market that wasn't built for them. That's an opinion. The table below is not.

You're buying clarity from a fractional CMO and capacity from an agency. That's the frame nearly every comparison piece lands on, and it's roughly right. A full-time hire gets you both, at a salary most small businesses can't carry. We wrote up the agency comparison in full, trade-offs included, in fractional CMO vs. marketing agency.

One more number for context. The guides above repeat a savings pitch: fractional runs one-third to one-fifth the cost of the full-time equivalent, which MarketerHire frames as $150K–$250K saved a year. True as far as it goes. But the savings math assumes you were ever going to pay a $200,000 marketing executive, and if you're reading this, you probably weren't. Compare against what you'd actually do instead, which is usually a junior hire or an agency retainer. Or nothing.

When a fractional CMO isn't worth it

Before product-market fit, don't hire one. If people aren't buying yet, the problem is the offer or the audience, and a retainer won't fix either. Buy a one-time plan at most, do the homework yourself, and come back later. We turn down pre-PMF retainer inquiries, and we'd rather tell you that here than on a call.

If you've already proven a channel and just need more hands on it, you don't need strategy by the month. Hire an agency or a good freelancer for that channel. Cheaper, and honestly a better fit.

And there's a structural failure worth knowing before you shop. Automate the Journey, whose piece on this question ranks for a reason, names the pattern: a fractional CMO "gives you the plan but doesn't run the work." You end up with a strategy deck and nobody to execute it. Ask any candidate who implements, by name, before you sign. Our answer is that the same team does both. Other good answers exist. No answer is a red flag.

Questions people ask next

Is a fractional CMO worth it for a small business?

Usually, once you're past roughly $500K in revenue and marketing has outgrown guesswork. upGrowth's hiring guides put the trigger between $500K and $2M. Before product-market fit, no. At that stage a plan you run yourself beats any retainer, including ours.

How many hours does a fractional CMO work?

Published guides from MarketerHire and Kalungi put it at 10 to 20 hours a week per client. That's enough to own the strategy and direct whoever executes. It is not enough to also do the hands-on work, so ask how execution gets staffed before you sign anything.

What does a fractional CMO actually do?

They own your marketing outcomes part-time: positioning, the plan, channel priorities, budget, and oversight of the people doing the work. A consultant advises and leaves. A fractional CMO is accountable for whether the number moves.

How much does a full-time CMO cost compared to a fractional one?

Full-time CMO compensation runs $175,000 to $300,000 and up before benefits, per MarketerHire and Chief Outsiders. Those same guides put fractional work at one-third to one-fifth of that cost. For most small businesses the real comparison is fractional versus nobody owning marketing at all.
