GoHighLevel vs HubSpot: which costs less, and for whom?

A head-to-head table, the real monthly cost at three list sizes, and a plain read on who each tool is for. Prices are from both companies’ own pages, checked October 2026.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

GoHighLevel costs less and fits local service businesses: $97 a month flat, unlimited contacts. HubSpot is easier to start and suits marketing-led teams, but its Professional tier runs $890 a month plus onboarding. At 5,000 contacts, list prices work out to about $97 versus $1,140 a month, before usage fees.

That gap is real, and it isn’t the whole story. The two tools are built for different owners. Here’s the head-to-head, the cost at three list sizes, and who should pick which. We set up GoHighLevel for clients, so we’ll show our math and mark our opinions.

How do GoHighLevel and HubSpot compare side by side?

What does each one really cost at 1,000, 5,000, and 10,000 contacts?

The two companies charge for different things. GoHighLevel doesn’t charge by contact. HubSpot’s Marketing Hub does, and only for contacts you mark as marketing contacts.

Here’s our math for a three-person team that sends email and runs follow-up automation. For HubSpot we used Marketing Hub, since that’s where the email and automation limits stop pinching. Professional includes 2,000 marketing contacts, and HubSpot’s product catalog lists extra contacts at $250 a month per block of 5,000 in that range. At Starter, 1,000 contacts and 3 seats cost $60 a month.

HubSpot figures use monthly billing and exclude the one-time $3,000 Professional onboarding. Annual billing drops the Professional base from $890 to $800 a month. GoHighLevel stays at $97 a month on its Starter plan, or $970 a year if you pay annually. Add Sales Hub seats for sequences and the HubSpot number climbs. Our $13,680 sits $1,200 a year below the $14,880 in our GoHighLevel cost breakdown, which comes from third-party comparisons. That gap is one Sales Hub Professional seat at $100 a month. Those same comparisons price GoHighLevel at $3,564 a year (its $297 plan), where we use the $97 plan. Different assumptions, same prices underneath.

At 1,000 contacts, HubSpot wins on price. At 5,000, it’s about twelve times GoHighLevel. We think that’s the honest summary, and it surprises people in both directions.

What does GoHighLevel add on top of the plan?

Usage. Per its LC Phone guide, US texts run $0.00747 per segment and a local number is $1.15 a month. Per its pricing guide, email is $0.675 per 1,000 sends. So 2,000 texts and 10,000 emails a month is about $23, before carrier fees.

The AI features cost more. Its pricing page lists AI Employee add-ons at $50 or $97 a month per sub-account. A busy account with AI on can land well above $100 a month in add-ons, and independent reviews put typical usage at $30 to $300 a month. Budget the plan plus a usage line, and watch the first two bills.

Who should pick GoHighLevel?

Service businesses where the lead arrives by phone, text, or form and the owner can’t always answer. Contractors, salons, clinics, cleaners. You get calendars, forms, missed-call text-back, review requests, and email and text in one account, which usually replaces several separate subscriptions.

It also suits owners comfortable with a blank canvas, or willing to pay someone to build the canvas once. It won’t hold your hand. The usual failure is a login, a 14-day trial, and nothing built.

When is HubSpot the better buy?

Teams with marketing in the mix. Professional services, B2B, software, anyone running a blog, landing pages, and email campaigns next to a sales pipeline. HubSpot lists more than 2,000 integrations, and its free tier gets two people started at $0. If your team already opens HubSpot every morning, stay put.

It’s also the lower-risk first CRM for a team that dislikes setup. The trade is the price ladder. Free and Starter are friendly. Professional is where automation, reporting, and sequences live, and it costs about $890 a month for Marketing or $100 per seat for Sales.

Who should skip both?

Solo owners under about 50 leads a month. A calendar link and a spreadsheet cost nothing, and the 56% of small businesses without a CRM in a 2023 Zoho and SMB Group survey aren’t all wrong. We walk through the other options, including Pipedrive, Zoho, and Jobber, in the best CRM for a small business.

Skip a switch, too, if your team likes what it has. That same survey found 29% of non-adopters blamed staff resistance to new software. Tool churn costs real weeks.

What do you give up with the cheaper tool?

Polish and reporting depth. Our opinion: GoHighLevel’s interface is busier, and its reports suit a service business better than a marketing team. HubSpot’s Professional tier lists 75 dashboards and custom reporting, and its Enterprise tier adds multi-touch revenue attribution. If you need to show a board which channel produced which dollar, that’s worth paying for. If you need to know which missed calls got a text within a minute, you don’t.

Honest warning, too. Cheap and flexible means you build more yourself. A $97 tool you never set up is the most expensive CRM in this comparison.

How hard is it to move from HubSpot to GoHighLevel?

The software move is easy. The data move isn’t. Export contacts, deals, and notes, then merge duplicates, map fields, and decide which tags survive. Add business-text registration with the carriers, and email domain authentication so messages reach inboxes. That’s the dull week. Do it before you turn on a single automation.

When we move clients off HubSpot, Keap, or a spreadsheet, we spot-check the new account against the old one before anything gets turned off. Nothing goes dark mid-switch. What else do owners ask about GoHighLevel and HubSpot?

Is GoHighLevel cheaper than HubSpot?

At most list sizes, yes. GoHighLevel is $97 a month flat with unlimited contacts. HubSpot Starter costs less at 1,000 contacts, about $60 a month for 3 seats. At 5,000 contacts, Marketing Hub Professional is about $1,140 a month. Add GoHighLevel’s metered usage before you compare.

Can GoHighLevel replace HubSpot?

For a service business, usually. GoHighLevel covers pipelines, calendars, forms, email, texting, and reviews. It’s weaker as a polished reporting tool for a marketing team, and it starts blank. If your team uses HubSpot’s reporting and ecosystem daily, replacing it costs real time.

Does HubSpot charge per contact?

Marketing Hub does, for marketing contacts only. Professional includes 2,000, then HubSpot’s product catalog lists $250 a month per extra block of 5,000 in that range. Contacts you don’t market to can stay non-marketing and aren’t billed. Sales Hub charges per seat instead.

Which is better for a small service business?

GoHighLevel, for most. Missed-call text-back, reminders, booking, and review requests come in one flat plan. HubSpot fits better if your leads come from content and email, and a sales team works them in a pipeline.

Does GoHighLevel charge extra for texts and email?

Yes. Texts, email, calls, and AI features are metered on top of the plan. Published rates are about $0.00747 per text segment and $0.675 per 1,000 emails, plus carrier fees. AI add-ons start at $50 a month per sub-account.

Do I need a consultant to set up GoHighLevel?

No, if you have the hours. Plan about a week to get it working and a couple of months to settle. Our setup sits inside monthly retainers of $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800, three months minimum.
