How much does a marketing consultant cost?

Hourly, project, and monthly ranges from published guides, our own prices beside them, and five questions for comparing quotes.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

Marketing consultants charge roughly $75 to $300 an hour, $5,000 to $50,000 a project, or $1,500 to $15,000 a month. Ours are published: $1,200, $2,400, or $4,800 a month, or $3,000 for a one-time strategy plan. Here’s what moves the number.

Full disclosure: we sell this. The market ranges below come from published guides, named inline, and several are written by firms that sell consulting too. Treat them as ranges, not quotes.

What do marketing consultants charge?

Three ways, and the model changes the price more than the person does.

Pricing model Typical range What you get Fits when
Hourly $75 to $300+ an hour Advice, reviews, a second opinion The job is short or the scope is unclear
Project $5,000 to $50,000+ A defined deliverable: an audit, a plan, a launch You know the end point and the date
Monthly retainer $1,500 to $15,000 Ongoing direction, and sometimes the doing Marketing needs steady attention

By the hour, Outerbox puts independent consultants at $75 to $250 and senior specialists or fractional leaders at $150 to $500. MarketerHire, drawing on pricing from 30,000+ matches on its own platform, sorts by experience: $100 to $150 for 2 to 5 years, $150 to $225 for 5 to 10 years, and $225 to $300+ past 10.

By the project, both guides land on $5,000 to $50,000+. The low end is a single campaign audit. The high end is a full go-to-market strategy.

By the month, Outerbox lists $1,500 to $10,000+ for narrow consulting support. MarketerHire lists $3,000 to $15,000 for 10 to 40 hours of work.

One detail worth knowing: MarketerHire reports that 68% of its engagements start hourly during a trial and then convert to a monthly retainer. Hourly is often the on-ramp, not the destination.

Smaller shops price lower. Credo’s consultant pricing survey (2019 data, so older than the guides above) put the average at $141.67 an hour. It found 73% of consultants had a retainer minimum under $3,000 a month, and 35% under $1,000.

What moves the price up or down?

Seniority first. Someone who has run marketing through two growth stages charges more than someone two years out of an agency job. That’s fair, and the gap in the MarketerHire numbers above is the proof.

Specialty second. A paid-search specialist and a brand strategist don’t cost the same, and neither should be asked to do the other’s job.

Scope third, and it’s the one owners miss. A consultant who tells you what to do costs less than one who also does it. A $4,000 plan nobody carries out costs more than a $4,000 plan someone helps you run. The most common budget leak isn’t the rate. It’s buying advice with nobody assigned to act on it. Outerbox suggests every proposal state where consulting ends and implementation begins. We agree.

For scale: the Bureau of Labor Statistics puts median pay for marketing managers at $165,780 a year (May 2025), salary alone. A retainer looks different next to that.

What are you really paying for?

Three things, and only one of them is hours. Judgment: someone who has seen your problem before and knows which fix comes first. Time: the hours in the quote, spent on your business. Accountability: a person who answers for whether the number moved.

Most quotes spell out the hours and wave at the other two. Ask about them directly. Ask, too, what sits inside the quote and what sits on top of it: ad spend, software, hosting, photography. Those lines are where a $3,000 month becomes a $4,500 one.

What should make you walk away?

A proposal with no deliverables, only activities. “Social media management” is an activity. “Twelve posts, a posting calendar, and a monthly report of calls from Instagram” is a deliverable.

Hourly billing with no cap. Long lock-ins with no exit. And any arrangement where the consultant holds your ad accounts, your domain, or your Google profile. You should own all of it from day one. If a consultant gets nervous when you ask, you have your answer.

How do you compare two quotes?

Put five questions to every consultant, in writing:

  • What exactly will I hold in my hands at the end of month one?
  • Who does the work, by name?
  • What numbers will the report show? Calls, customers, and booked work beat impressions.
  • What do I own if we stop? Your site, accounts, and data should be yours.
  • What’s the minimum commitment, and how do I leave?

Two quotes that answer all five are comparable. Two that don’t are guesses, and the cheaper guess usually becomes the expensive one.

When isn’t a consultant worth the money?

When you’re too early. If $1,200 a month would strain the business, a retainer isn’t your next move. Work out a number you can sustain first. Our guide to what small-business marketing should cost shows the math.

When you already know what to do. If you have the plan and need hands, hire a freelancer or an agency for the channel. We say this on sales calls too.

When nobody’s buying yet. Before product-market fit, a paid plan mostly says “go sell.” We’d rather give you the homework free.

What else do owners ask about consultant pricing?

Is it cheaper to hire a marketing consultant or an agency?

It depends on what you’re buying. A consultant sells judgment, and an agency sells production. Agencies rarely publish rates, so ask each one for a written scope and compare what you’d hold at the end of month one. Our full comparison is Fractional CMO vs. marketing agency.

How much should a small business budget for a marketing consultant?

Start from your revenue, not from the consultant’s rate card. Our free Budget Calculator turns revenue into a monthly marketing range with the math shown. If your range falls under $1,200 a month, a one-time strategy plan plus your own effort usually fits better than a retainer.

Do marketing consultants charge hourly or monthly?

Both, plus by the project. Hourly suits short or uncertain jobs, retainers suit ongoing work, and projects suit a defined deliverable. We don’t bill by the hour. Our work is a monthly retainer or a $3,000 one-time engagement, and one-off builds are quoted in writing first.

What’s the difference between a marketing consultant and a fractional CMO?

A consultant advises and usually leaves. A fractional CMO is accountable for whether the number moves, part-time and ongoing. MarketerHire puts fractional CMO retainers at $3,000 to $15,000 a month. Ours run $1,200 to $4,800.

Are there setup or onboarding fees?

Not with us. No onboarding fee, no setup charge, no surprise line items. One-time builds like a new website are quoted in writing before anything starts.
