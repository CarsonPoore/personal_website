How much do Google Ads cost a small business?

Real 2026 numbers by industry, what a $1,000 budget actually buys, how to pick your own number from what a customer is worth, and when the honest answer is to wait.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

Most small businesses pay about $2 to $10 a click and $27 to $132 a lead on Google Ads, depending on the industry. At the 2026 all-industry averages ($5.42 a click, $66.69 a lead), $1,000 a month buys about 185 clicks and 15 leads. Someone running the account for you costs extra.

That average is a fine place to start and a bad place to stop. A plumber and a restaurant sit at opposite ends of the same chart. What you can afford to pay for a lead depends on what a customer is worth to you, so we’ll do that math below with numbers you can swap for your own.

What does a click and a lead cost on Google Ads in 2026?

LocaliQ, the company behind WordStream, publishes the benchmarks most people quote. Its 2026 search advertising benchmarks put the average cost per click at $5.42, the average click-through rate at 6.64%, and the average conversion rate at 8.18%. The average cost per lead is $66.69. LocaliQ also reports that cost per lead fell in every industry it tracks, the first time in five years that happened.

Your industry moves those numbers a lot. Here are the ones local owners ask about, with what $1,000 a month buys at each lead price.

Read it sideways. A legal click costs nearly five times a restaurant click. Real estate clicks are cheap and leads are not, because most clicks never turn into a conversation. (The last column is our arithmetic: $1,000 divided by the cost per lead.)

One caution. A lead in these benchmarks is a conversion Google counted, usually a call or a form. It isn’t a booked job. Some leads are price shoppers or people outside your area. That’s why your close rate matters more than any row in the table.

What decides what you actually pay?

Every search runs a small auction. Your bid is the most you’ll pay, and Google says you often pay less. It also says higher quality ads can lower your cost per click, because quality feeds into Ad Rank alongside your bid. So a sharper ad and a better page can beat a bigger budget.

Three things are in your hands:

  • How closely the ad, the search, and the landing page match. Same service, same town, phone number up top.
  • The searches you refuse to pay for. Job hunters and do-it-yourselfers click a lot and never call. A negative keyword list stops that spend.
  • Where and when ads run. A budget aimed at the towns you actually serve goes further than one spread across the whole metro.

Competition is the one you don’t control. If five Indianapolis HVAC companies bid on the same emergency searches, the price goes up for everyone. Our take: a cheap click isn’t the goal. A cheap booked job is. We’d take a $12 click on “emergency water heater replacement Greenwood” over a $2 click on “how to flush a water heater.”

How much should you budget each month?

Start from the job, not the benchmark. You need three numbers: what an average job earns you after costs, how many leads it takes to book one, and how many jobs you want a month.

Here’s an example with made-up numbers, so swap in yours. A water heater job keeps $600 after parts and labor. You book 1 of every 4 leads. So a lead is worth about $150 to you. The home-services benchmark is $91 a lead, which leaves room for profit and for someone to manage the account. Want 10 booked jobs a month? That’s 40 leads. At $91, about $3,640 in ad spend.

If that number is bigger than your budget, start smaller on purpose. We think $500 a month is about the floor. At $8.33 a click, $500 buys roughly 60 clicks. That’s too few to learn which searches turn into calls, and you’ll spend months guessing.

One mechanical note. Google thinks in daily budgets. Divide your monthly number by 30.4, so $1,000 becomes about $33 a day. Google may spend up to twice your daily budget on a given day, but won’t charge more than 30.4 times your daily budget in a month. A busy Tuesday isn’t a billing error.

What does it cost to have someone run the ads?

It depends on how the person charges. One agency’s 2026 pricing guide, from Outer Box Design, says percentage-of-spend fees commonly land around 10% to 20% of monthly ad spend. For small accounts it lists flat fees of $500 to $2,000 a month on $1,000 to $5,000 of spend. That’s one agency’s view, not an industry survey.

When are Google Ads not worth it?

We think most local businesses buy Google Ads too early. Ads amplify what’s already there, and four things have to be true first.

Your offer gives a stranger a reason to pick you over the next tab. Someone answers the phone and the form within minutes. Your Google Business Profile is complete, with recent reviews, because people check it after they click. And you can tell which calls came from an ad. If any of those is missing, the cheaper fix comes first. Our article on why businesses don’t show up on Google covers the profile side, and what a small business should spend on marketing covers where ads fit in the whole budget.

Also skip ads if the math doesn’t close. If an average customer is worth $60 to you and a lead costs $67, no tuning fixes that. Pick a channel where a customer costs less than they’re worth.

What else do owners ask about Google Ads costs?

How much should a small business spend on Google Ads per month?

There’s no universal number. Work backward from your job value: what a lead is worth, times the leads you need. We think $500 a month is about the floor for learning anything, and many local service businesses land higher. The Budget Calculator gives a starting range.

Why is my cost per click so high?

Usually one of three reasons: your industry is expensive (legal clicks average $9.87 and restaurants $2.05, per LocaliQ’s 2026 benchmarks), competitors are bidding on the same searches, or your ads and landing page don’t match what people typed. The third one you can fix.

How long until Google Ads brings in leads?

Clicks start within days of launch. Useful results take longer, because the first weeks go to finding which searches turn into booked jobs and cutting the ones that don’t. That’s why we ask for three months before you judge, and why a 30-day test proves little.

Will Google charge me more than my daily budget?

On a given day, yes, up to twice your daily budget. Over a month, no. Google says you won’t be charged more than 30.4 times your average daily budget. A $33 daily budget can cost $66 on a busy day and still stays under $1,000 for the month.

Can I run Google Ads myself?

Yes, and plenty of owners do. It works if you can check the account weekly, keep negative keywords current, and track calls. It stops working when nobody looks at it for a month. If you can’t commit that time, paying someone usually costs less than the wasted clicks.
