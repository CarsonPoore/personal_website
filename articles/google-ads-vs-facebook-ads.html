Google Ads or Facebook Ads: which fits a local business?

Search catches people who are already looking. Social reaches people who weren’t. Here are the 2026 costs side by side, which kind of business fits which, and the honest case for neither.

By Carson Poore Last updated: Oct 9, 2026

For most local businesses, Google Ads wins first. It reaches people already searching for what you sell, so it fits urgent work like repairs, dentists, and lawyers. Facebook and Instagram ads interrupt people who weren’t looking, and suit businesses that sell on looks or repeat visits. Leads average $67 on Google and $27 on Facebook, but they aren’t the same kind of lead.

Picture a homeowner whose water heater just quit. She types “water heater repair near me.” That’s search. Now picture the same person on a Saturday, scrolling past a friend’s vacation photos, when your ad for a tune-up appears. That’s interruption. Same person, very different mood, and very different odds that she picks up the phone.

What’s the real difference between Google and Facebook ads?

Intent. Google ads show up when someone types a question, so they’re answering a need that already exists. Facebook and Instagram ads show up in a feed, so they’re creating attention for something the person hadn’t thought about yet. Neither is better in the abstract. They do different jobs.

Both audiences are big. Pew Research Center’s 2025 survey found 71% of US adults use Facebook and half use Instagram. But when people go looking for a local business, they start somewhere else. In BrightLocal’s 2026 research, 52% of consumers began their most recent local search on Google, and 71% used Google Search at some point along the way. Three in four used more than one channel, so nobody stays in a single lane.

Targeting differs too. Google targets what people type. Facebook targets who people are: where they live, their age, what they follow. That’s why a restaurant can reach everyone within three miles on Facebook, while a plumber is better off waiting for the person who typed “burst pipe.”

What do they cost side by side?

LocaliQ publishes 2026 benchmarks for both. The Google figures come from its search advertising benchmarks, and the Facebook figures from its Facebook advertising benchmarks for lead campaigns.

The headline says Facebook is cheaper per lead, and mostly it is. Look at the last row, though. For beauty and personal care, Google leads cost less. Industry beats rule of thumb.

Don’t stop at cost per lead. A Facebook lead is often a two-tap form from someone who was scrolling. A Google lead is often a phone call from someone who just searched. We think those are different products, and the cheaper one isn’t automatically the better buy. The only fair comparison is cost per booked job, and you can only see that if you track it. (Our article on measuring marketing ROI shows how.) Also note LocaliQ’s two reports use different sets of advertisers, so treat the table as a guide, not a head-to-head test.

Should you run both?

Eventually, maybe. At first, no. Our opinion: with $1,000 a month, put all of it in one channel for three months. Split it two ways and neither gets enough clicks to teach you anything.

For most local service businesses we’d pick Google first and add Facebook later, to remind people who already visited your site or to promote something timely. For a restaurant or a salon, we’d flip the order. If you’re not sure, a free call settles it. We run Google Ads and handle your social media posting and replies, both inside our published retainers. We don’t manage Facebook or Instagram ads. If they’re the better fit for you, we’ll say so and point you to someone who does.

When aren’t ads on either one worth it?

Ads amplify what’s there. If your Google profile is thin, calls roll to voicemail, or your offer doesn’t give a stranger a reason to pick you, paid clicks leak. What else do owners ask about Google and Facebook ads?

Is Google Ads or Facebook Ads cheaper for a local business?

Facebook usually costs less per click and per lead. LocaliQ’s 2026 benchmarks show $27.39 a lead on Facebook against $66.69 on Google. But the leads differ, and some industries flip the result: beauty and personal care leads cost less on Google. Compare cost per booked job, not cost per lead.

Do Facebook ads work for service businesses like plumbers and electricians?

They can, but they work harder for you when the need is planned, like a remodel or a tune-up, than when it’s urgent. Someone with a flooded basement searches Google. Someone thinking about a new water heater next spring may respond to a Facebook ad.

Can I run Google and Facebook ads on a $500 budget?

You can, but we’d pick one. At $500 a month, a split gives each channel too few clicks to learn from. Put it all on the channel where your customers make the decision, run it for three months, and judge by calls and booked jobs.

Is Instagram a separate ad platform from Facebook?

No. Facebook and Instagram ads are bought through the same Meta ad account, and you choose where they appear. The audiences differ, though. Pew’s 2025 survey found 71% of US adults use Facebook and half use Instagram, and Instagram skews much younger.

Which gets leads faster, Google Ads or Facebook Ads?

Both start delivering clicks within days. Google leads tend to be ready to buy now, so a good account can book work quickly. Facebook leads often need a follow-up call or text to warm up. Either way, give the first three months to tuning before you judge.
